\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{What the three studies say together.} The models' value on sparse daily counts is in the size of
the positive days: their gain over a trailing mean sits where the local mean overpredicts a positive day,
and their positive law beats a shifted Poisson wherever that law is misspecified and an empirical law on
dense series. Their own probability of a zero day is at least as good as a context-only estimate on dense
series, under the quantile score in all three settings and under the occurrence Brier score on NYC 311 and at
a week on npm. This is confirmatory on NYC 311, through the dense stratum's own six tests of the dynamic
occurrence; on npm and the grid the confirmatory families are pooled, none favors the fixed substitution and
two of six npm tests and all three grid tests reject against it, and their reading as the dense answer rests
on the declared secondary strata. On sparse series the answer depends on the domain: the typical sparse npm
series favors a context estimate at a week, under all three scores and for the TSB hurdle as much as for the
Jeffreys estimate, and not at a month; on NYC 311's sparse stratum one of twelve registered tests favors it
and ten of twelve medians are exactly zero. On series that are zero nine days in ten nothing about
occurrence can be read, because the completion sets the models' occurrence probability and the quantile
score ties. The dynamic hurdle beats the empirical count hurdle in 14 of 18 registered tests on NYC 311,
three with a median of exactly zero; on sparse and very sparse series that contrast owes as much to the
swapped occurrence as to the positive law.

\paragraph{The two readings of the accounting trade.} The hurdle's positive law $G_+$ is the model's completed
marginal law truncated at one, not a size forecast the model made for positive days; context zero mass laid
over a law that already sits low on positive days counts the zeros twice, and zero-day loss down with
underprediction up by more is what that predicts. Two readings of the net sign were open after Study 2: that
the context estimate is the worse occurrence forecast, and that the truncated law is not the model's size
forecast, so that any added zero mass hurts. The record supports the first on NYC 311's dense series, where
the dynamic occurrence is worse on the Brier score itself in all six rows and the fourth cell against \hc{},
which differs from it in the occurrence alone, puts the model's own occurrence ahead of the Jeffreys estimate
on 62 to 73 of the 100 dense series under a count law that cannot count zeros twice, and at a week on the
grid for Chronos-2 and TimesFM 3. It supports the second at a month on npm and the grid, where the Jeffreys
estimate is as good as or better than the model's zero mass on the Brier score and under the count law, yet
laid over the models' truncated positive laws it raises \qs{} against the control on 57 to 66 of the 100
dense npm series and on 24 or 25 of the 30 grid blocks. The fourth cell against the control differs from it
in the positive law alone and measures the model's law against a shifted Poisson, far worse on dense NYC 311
series by 0.2944 to 0.325 on the mean; it says nothing about either reading. Which reading operates depends
on the panel and the horizon, and nothing beyond that is attributed.

\paragraph{What was expected, and what surprised.} The design expected the sparse npm reading to reproduce
on a second domain; it did not, and the paper's first draft gave two readings of that outcome with equal
weight. Three things in the record changed that. The first is the completion floor, in the registered rule
from the start and not seen until the very sparse ties were counted: on a series that is zero nine days in
ten the registered ``model's own zero mass'' is a constant between 0.0607 and 0.1, so the very sparse question
the paper had called open is not one the registered construction can ask, and the ties were foreseeable from
the definition. The second is that the sparse npm week is a property of the context estimate as such, since
the TSB hurdle wins the same series, and of the week rather than the month. The third is the registered
dynamic count, which beats every foundation model on the grid and loses to them on every natural panel: with
the occurrence held equal, the foundation models' positive laws underpredict the grid's positive days more
than its negative binomial does (Appendix~\ref{app:s2tables}). The threshold of 0.5 between the regimes was
carried from Study 2's sampling strata, not derived; Figure~\ref{fig:signature} suggests that where the
substitution starts to help is domain-specific.

\paragraph{What would be done differently.} The next study of this question registers the occurrence log or
Brier score and the discrete ranked probability score as its primaries, keeps a model output that can express
an occurrence probability below one in ten, such as Chronos-2's own 21 quantile levels, a mixture or sample
paths, registers TSB and iETS beside the count baselines, chooses the regime threshold from a per-series
curve, and runs on a prospective window; that is one study, and the one a reader of the intermittent-demand
literature would ask for. Short of it, the piece a reviewer could ask for is a registered replication of the
sparse npm week on target days not yet read, six tests under the occurrence Brier score, which is held in
reserve.

\paragraph{What the paper claims.} The paper claims the registered combination and nothing broader: a
context-only or dynamic occurrence probability substituted for the zero mass of frozen Chronos-2 and TimesFM
forecasts, their completed positive law kept, judged by a proper quantile score against a completed-unwrapped
control and matched count hurdles, with a dynamic count baseline beside them, on a hash-sampled panel of daily
npm download counts, on the registered synthetic grid and, stratum by stratum, on a stratified panel of daily
NYC 311 service-request counts, with the mean and the typical series reported side by side; Study 1's GitHub star counts enter through the raw comparison alone.
