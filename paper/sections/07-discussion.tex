\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{What the three studies say together.} The models' value on sparse daily counts is in the size of
the positive days: their gain over a trailing mean sits on positive days, where the local mean overpredicts
on GitHub and npm and underpredicts on NYC 311, and their positive law beats a shifted Poisson wherever that
law is misspecified and an empirical law on dense series. Their own probability of a zero day is at least as good as a context-only estimate on dense
series, under the quantile score in all three settings and under the occurrence Brier score on NYC 311 and at
a week on npm. This is confirmatory on NYC 311, through the dense stratum's own six tests of the dynamic
occurrence; on npm and the grid the confirmatory families are pooled, none favors the fixed substitution and
two of six npm tests and all three grid tests reject against it, and their reading as the dense answer rests
on the declared secondary strata. On sparse series the answer depends on the domain: the typical sparse npm
series favors a context estimate at a week, for the TSB hurdle as much as for the Jeffreys estimate, and not
at a month by the within-stratum test; on NYC 311's sparse stratum one of six T1 tests favors it and four of
six medians are exactly zero; and the grid's cells with $z = 0.95$ at a month favor it. On the natural panels' series
that are zero nine days in ten nothing about occurrence can be read, because the completion sets the models'
occurrence probability and the quantile score ties. T2 rejects for the dynamic hurdle against the empirical
count hurdle in 14 of 18 registered tests on NYC 311, three of them by rank sum with a median of
exactly zero; on sparse series that contrast owes at least as much to the swapped occurrence as to the
positive law, and on very sparse series everything.

\paragraph{The two readings of the accounting trade.} Of the two readings of the net sign left open after
Study 2, that the context estimate is the worse occurrence forecast and that the hurdle's truncated law is
not the model's size forecast, the record supports the first on dense NYC 311 series and at a week on the
grid, and the second at a month on the grid and, on the Brier score, on npm, post hoc there
(Appendix~\ref{app:s3tables}); nothing beyond that is attributed.

\paragraph{What was expected, and what surprised.} The design expected the sparse npm reading to reproduce
on a second domain; it did not, and three things in the record explain why. The first is the completion
floor, in the registered rule from the start: before the run its first stage counted 74 of 100 very sparse
series on the Jeffreys fallback and expected many exact ties. On a series that is zero nine days in ten
the registered ``model's own zero mass'' is a constant between 0.0607 and 0.1, so the very sparse question is
not one the registered construction can ask, and the ties were foreseeable from the definition. The second
is that the sparse npm week is a property of the context estimate as such, since the TSB hurdle wins the
same series, and of the week rather than the month. The third is the registered dynamic count, which beats
every foundation model on the grid at a month and loses to them on npm and NYC 311, on the mean and by
count: with the occurrence held equal, the foundation models' positive laws underpredict the grid's positive
days more than its negative binomial does (Appendix~\ref{app:s2tables}). The threshold of 0.5 between the regimes was
carried from Study 2's sampling strata, not derived; Figure~\ref{fig:signature} suggests that where the
substitution starts to help is domain-specific.

\paragraph{What would be done differently.} The next study of this question registers the occurrence log or
Brier score and the discrete ranked probability score as its primaries, keeps a model output that can express
an occurrence probability below one in ten, such as Chronos-2's own 21 quantile levels, a mixture or sample
paths, registers TSB and iETS beside the count baselines, chooses the regime threshold from a per-series
curve, and runs on a prospective window; that is one study, and the one a reader of the intermittent-demand
literature would ask for. Short of it, the piece a reviewer could ask for is a registered replication of the
sparse npm week on target days not yet read, six tests under the occurrence Brier score, which is held in
reserve.

\paragraph{What the paper claims.} The paper claims the registered combination and nothing broader: a
context-only or dynamic occurrence probability substituted for the zero mass of frozen Chronos-2 and TimesFM
forecasts, their completed positive law kept, judged by a proper quantile score against a completed-unwrapped
control and matched count hurdles, with a dynamic count baseline beside them, on a hash-sampled panel of daily
npm download counts, on the registered synthetic grid and, stratum by stratum, on a stratified panel of daily
NYC 311 service-request counts, with the mean and the typical series reported side by side; Study 1's GitHub star counts enter through the raw comparison alone.
