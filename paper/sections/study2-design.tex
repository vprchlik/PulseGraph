\section{Study 2: the registered design}
\label{sec:s2design}

What follows is the design as registered on 2026-09-14 at commit \texttt{df55867}, as it stands after two
dated additions at the protocol's end: the addition of 2026-10-12, committed at \texttt{8db0d73}, which
records the lapses of September 2026 and fixes the families of this paper, and the addition of 2026-10-15,
committed at \texttt{5be2ce9}, which fixes the availability rule for one secondary score. Both additions
were decided before any Study 2 target value was accessed; the status file each cites records that no
target had been opened. Nothing above either addition's heading changed, and the registered bytes of Study 1
and of the Study 2 addition remain byte prefixes of the protocol file.

\paragraph{Domains.} The registration names three external domain slots: package downloads, with npm as
the primary source and PyPI through ClickPy as its sole operational fallback; NYC 311 requests by problem
type and community board; and English Wikipedia pageviews, a domain with known training-source overlap for
all three model families. Each slot aims at 300 series, 100 in each of three context-zero-fraction strata,
$[0, 0.5)$, $[0.5, 0.9)$ and $[0.9, 1]$, computed on the last 90 context days; all-zero contexts are
eligible. The protocol calls this a balanced stress-test panel, not an estimate of a platform's natural
prevalence of sparsity. The addition of 2026-10-12 defers NYC 311 and Wikipedia to a later run, for calendar
and resource reasons decided before any target value was accessed, so this paper's external component is the
packages domain alone (Section~\ref{sec:s2panel}). npm's download counts measure tarball requests as npm
counts them, including mirrors and automated clients, not human adoption.

\paragraph{Frame and sampling.} The npm frame is the frozen inventory of package names, scopes included,
from the registry's replication feed, with a recorded creation time before the context start $A$; no
popularity search, download leaderboard or other popularity filter is permitted. Sampling is by hash:
with selection seed 20260914, each key's digest is the SHA-256 of the canonical JSON array of the seed, the
domain, a stage label and the key, and keys are sorted by digest. With stage ``candidate'' the first
$\min(10{,}000, N)$ metadata-eligible keys form a fixed candidate pool; the addition of 2026-10-12 reads
this rule as a walk of the inventory in hash order that requests creation metadata only until 10,000
eligible packages are found. The context of every candidate is inspected only from $A$ through the day
before the first cutoff, for completeness, and its last 90 days for the stratum. Within each stratum, with
stage ``selection'', the first 100 complete candidates in hash order are taken, keeping the full order, the
candidate-pool inclusion probability, the frame and stratum sizes and every exclusion with its reason. A
stratum with fewer than 100 complete candidates keeps what it has: no slot transfers between strata, no
larger pool and no alternative filter, and a reduced panel is reported as such. Acceptance is frozen on
context and source-coverage metadata before any target value is exposed; a later incomplete target stops
the domain rather than replacing or deleting a series, and no external confirmatory inference runs on a
failed domain. Completeness means that every required calendar day has verified exposure; missing records
are never zero-filled.

\paragraph{Calendar.} The retrospective calendar has context start $A$ = 2025-09-01, common endpoint $E$ =
2026-08-31 and $K = 3$ cutoffs at $c_j = E - (30j - 1)$ days for $j = 3, 2, 1$: 2026-06-03, 2026-07-03 and
2026-08-02, with 275, 305 and 335 context days. Context stops the day before the cutoff; the target block
runs from the cutoff for 7 or 30 days, so the three 30-day blocks partition the trailing 90 days and do not
overlap, and the 7-day blocks share the same origins. Selection strata are computed on $c_1 - 90$ through
$c_1 - 1$, 2026-03-05 through 2026-06-02, and frozen for all origins. Package day labels are UTC. No model
sees another series or any covariate, and no horizon is counted as an independent entity.

\paragraph{Models and variants.} The three foundation models are the Study 1 pins: \texttt{amazon/chronos-2}
at \texttt{29ec3766d36d6f73f0696f85560a422f50e8498c}, \texttt{google/timesfm-3.0-pytorch} at
\texttt{43046b85ec22d584a13f8098c2ed39c889e129c2} and \texttt{google/timesfm-2.5-200m-pytorch} at
\texttt{1d952420fba87f3c6dee4f240de0f1a0fbc790e3}, run univariate at batch size one on one GPU. For each
model and origin one base forecast is saved and three variants are scored from it, with no refitting:
\emph{raw}, the nine native deciles after Study 1's clipping and crossing repair, with no invented tails;
\emph{completed}, the count distribution obtained from those deciles by the fixed completion below, which is
the control; and \emph{hurdle}, the same completed law with its zero mass replaced by the context-only
occurrence estimate.

\paragraph{Completion.} From the nine repaired deciles $q_{0.1}, \dots, q_{0.9}$ the quantile function
$Q(u)$ is the linear interpolation through $(0, 0)$ and the nine knots for $0 \le u \le 0.9$, and for
$0.9 < u < 1$ an exponential upper tail $Q(u) = q_{0.9} + s \log(0.1 / (1 - u))$ with scale
$s = \max(1, q_{0.9} - q_{0.8})$, which has a finite first moment. The count is
$Y = \max(0, \lfloor Q(U) + 0.5 \rfloor)$ for $U$ uniform on $(0, 1)$, half-up rounding, and the
right-continuous count CDF $F(k) = P(Y \le k)$ carries the atoms that flat segments produce. Masses and
quantiles are computed by deterministic inversion, not by sampling the tails. The completed control is
exactly $F$. Whenever $F(0) < 1$ the hurdle variant uses the model's own positive conditional law
$G_+(k) = [F(k) - F(0)] / [1 - F(0)]$ for $k \ge 1$ and the forecast $H(0) = 1 - p$,
$H(k) = 1 - p + p\, G_+(k)$, with $p$ the 28-day Jeffreys estimate below; its quantile is zero for
$\tau \le 1 - p$ and otherwise the inverse of $G_+$ at $(\tau - 1 + p) / p$. Only when $F(0) = 1$ is
$G_+$ a point mass at one, and that condition is recorded. The wrapper changes occurrence and keeps the
completed positive law whenever one exists, so the hurdle and completed variants differ in nothing else.

\paragraph{Baselines.} Seven baselines are scored at every origin. \lm{} and \aets{} are the Study 1
implementations. \texttt{nb\_local\_level\_28} is Study 1's negative-binomial local level with the last-28
mean and full-context moment dispersion. \texttt{poisson\_28} is a Poisson with the last-28-day mean.
\hc{} is the fixed count hurdle: occurrence $p = (n_{+} + 1/2) / 29$ from the number of positive days
$n_{+}$ among the last 28 context days, constant over the horizon, and positive law
$1 + \text{Poisson}(\lambda)$ with $\lambda$ the mean of $y - 1$ over those positive days;
\texttt{hurdle\_empirical\_28} uses
the same occurrence estimate with the empirical distribution of the positive values, declared secondary.
With fewer than two positive days in the last 28, both count hurdles use a point mass at one for the
positive law while the occurrence estimate stands; the foundation-model hurdle never uses that rule.
\texttt{dynamic\_count} is a discounted Beta-Bernoulli and Gamma-Poisson hurdle: four parameters start at
$0.5$, are multiplied by $0.98$ before each context observation and updated by it, and at lead $l$ give
occurrence $a_B / (a_B + b_B)$ and a shifted negative-binomial positive size with the discount compounded to
$0.98^{\,l-1}$; it falls back to \hc{} with fewer than two recent positives. The count hurdles and the
dynamic count are the registered matched comparators: the same occurrence rule as the foundation-model
hurdle with a positive law that owes nothing to a foundation model. Current reference implementations of
hurdle-count smoothers and of a pooled hurdle with forgetting exist outside foundation models
\citep{damato2026, bai2026}; neither is run here.

\paragraph{The confirmatory family.} The registration declared one external family of 54 tests: for each of
three domains, three models and two horizons, the hurdle against its own completed control, the hurdle
against \hc{}, and the raw deciles against \lm{}, that is $3 \times 3 \times 2 \times 3 = 54$. The addition
of 2026-10-12 fixes this paper's external family as the eighteen packages-domain tests E01 to E18, exactly as
registered and listed in Table~\ref{tab:s2family}, with one Benjamini-Hochberg adjustment at $q = 0.05$ over
those eighteen. The family is eighteen because the two deferred domains contribute no test before this
paper, because the registered rule that only the holdout's npm-unavailability clause may reduce a family is
amended for this one case by the addition, and because tests E19 to E54 keep their registered definitions
and form, when NYC 311 and Wikipedia are run, one family of 36 with its own single correction; they are not
merged back into a 54-test correction after the eighteen have been seen. The settings are Study 1's: the
paired difference is model minus reference, so negative favors the model; the mean carries a pointwise 95
percent percentile interval from 20,000 bootstrap replicates of whole series (bootstrap seed 20260917); a
two-sided approximate Wilcoxon signed-rank test on series averages omits zero differences and reports $p =
1$ when every difference is zero; medians, wins, ties and losses and complete denominators are reported;
inference is conditional on the fixed dates. Every other contrast, the strata, the scaled score, the Brier
score, the CRPS, the PIT, skill, concentration and the zero-day and positive-day accounting are secondary and
add no test; the secondary scores, the seeds, the gate, the holdout and the contamination ledger are given
in Appendix~\ref{app:s2design}. The tuples of Table~\ref{tab:s2family} are committed as a declaration file that the execution
gate compares to the protocol before a run.

\paragraph{The synthetic grid.} The synthetic component separates occurrence, positive size and persistence
in a generator whose law is known. With $\pi = 1 - z$, the occurrence indicator $Z_t$ is a stationary
Markov Bernoulli chain with $P(Z_t = 1 \mid Z_{t-1} = a) = \pi + \rho\,(a - \pi)$ and $Z_0 \sim
\text{Bernoulli}(\pi)$; independently $X_t \sim \text{Poisson}(\mu_{+} - 1)$ and $Y_t = Z_t (1 + X_t)$. The
grid is the full product of $z \in \{0.2, 0.8, 0.95\}$, $\mu_{+} \in \{1, 5, 25\}$ and $\rho \in \{0,
0.8\}$: 18 cells, each with 30 independent replicates of 270 observations, the first context 180 days,
origins at indices 180, 210 and 240, horizons 7 and 30, so 540 series in all. Within a replicate the same
base uniforms serve every cell, as common random numbers, with separate streams for occurrence and size;
replicates are independent of one another. The generator manifest, with the synthetic root seed 20260918
and the per-replicate streams, is committed and must equal the generated grid before any series is
produced. The same models, completion, baselines and scores as the external study apply. An oracle that
knows the generator parameters and the last observed $Z$, with occurrence $\pi + \rho^{\,l} (Z_{\text{last}}
- \pi)$ at lead $l$ and positive law $1 + \text{Poisson}(\mu_{+} - 1)$, is a diagnostic: its CDF mass,
quantile inversion and expected pinball optimality were verified against the generator's known law before
any model run, it need not win every finite sample, and it is neither a competitor nor a confirmatory
contrast. The synthetic family is six tests at horizon 30, S01 to S06 (Table~\ref{tab:s2synfamily}): each
model's hurdle against its own completed control and against \hc{}, pooling all 18 cells with equal cell
weight, one Benjamini-Hochberg adjustment at $q = 0.05$. Origins are averaged within each replicate and
cell, then cells within replicate; the paired test and the bootstrap use the 30 replicate blocks, each
carrying all 18 cells, with the external family's settings. Seven-day scores and individual cells are
secondary, and no cell or generator was chosen because it reproduces Study 1.
