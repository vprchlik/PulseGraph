\paragraph{Against a shifted Poisson.} On npm the model's completed positive law beats the shifted Poisson
fitted to the last 28 days in all six registered cells, on the mean, the median and the rank test, with
adjusted $p$ from $2.66 \times 10^{-5}$ to $3.19 \times 10^{-25}$. The mean difference favors the variant in
every cell, from -5,090 to -6,918 (carried by \texttt{aggregate-error}), with every interval below zero. Every
median is negative too, from -0.006349 to -0.0716. The occurrence Brier difference is exactly zero for every
series in these six rows, since the two share the Jeffreys estimate, so the gain is in the positive law. By
stratum the win is a dense one, on 80 to 94 of the 100 dense series and on 29 to 63 of the 100 sparse ones.
This suggests a win over a comparator that cannot fit thousands of downloads a day.

On the grid, where the shifted Poisson is the generator's own law up to its estimated mean, the registered
contrast goes the other way or is not rejected. The Chronos-2 and TimesFM 2.5 hurdles score higher than the
count hurdle and reject, and the TimesFM 3 hurdle's interval includes zero (Table~\ref{tab:pos}).

From the other side, the fourth cell leads to the same conclusion, post hoc on npm and the grid and as a
declared secondary on NYC 311. With a shifted Poisson in place of the model's own positive law and the model's
own occurrence kept, the forecast is far worse on dense series. It is higher than the control on 79 to 94 of
the 100 dense npm series with medians of 0.39 to 0.56. On the dense NYC 311 series it is far worse too, with
mean differences of 0.2944 to 0.325 and higher on 70 to 91 of the 100. Even where the generator draws positive
days from a shifted Poisson, it is higher on 19 to 28 of the 30 grid blocks.

\paragraph{Against an empirical law.} The empirical hurdle is the count law that can fit a dense series.
Against it the model's law keeps its value on dense series and not in general. On npm the fixed hurdle is
lower on 63 to 72 of the 100 dense series and on 33 to 57 of the 100 sparse.

The registered T2 tests on NYC 311 compare the dynamic hurdle with the empirical hurdle, so they change the
occurrence and the positive law at once. Here 14 of the 18 T2 tests reject, all for the dynamic hurdle, three
of them with a median of exactly zero, leaning to the dynamic hurdle by rank sum. In the dense stratum all six
rows reject, with the dynamic hurdle lower on 72 to 87 of the 100 series and medians from -0.05608 to
-0.06753. Five of six reject in the sparse stratum. In the very sparse stratum the three horizon-30 rows
reject (R28, R32 and R36), each with 22 series lower, 77 tied and 1 higher and a median of exactly zero. Their
side therefore comes from the rank sums alone.

A declared secondary holds the occurrence at the Jeffreys estimate, the fixed hurdle against the empirical
hurdle. It puts the dense advantage on the positive law, lower on 75 to 88 of 100. On the sparse series it is
mixed, with every median zero, and it ties on 96 to 99 of 100 very sparse series. The model's positive law
therefore keeps its value against a count law on the dense NYC 311 series. A plausible reading of the
secondary is that the T2 rejections on sparse series owe at least as much to the dynamic occurrence as to the
positive law, and that on very sparse series they owe everything to it.

\paragraph{The dynamic count and the TSB hurdle.} The registered dynamic count is a four-parameter discounted
hurdle with no foundation model. At a month it is the best forecaster on the grid after the oracle, which no
forecaster scores below in 17 of the 18 cells. Its mean \qs{} at horizon 30 is 1.721 against 1.754 to 1.792
for the three completed models, 1.819 for the count hurdle and 1.648 for the oracle. By replicate block it is
lower than each completed model in 18 to 24 of 30 blocks. However, at a week five model variants have a lower
mean (Appendix~\ref{app:s2tables}). On npm it loses, and the completed models are lower on 108 to 146 of 209
series. It is far behind on dense NYC 311 series and level elsewhere. The TSB hurdle stands where the
registered count baselines stand. It is level with the count hurdle on dense NYC 311 series and ahead of it on
dense npm series (Table~\ref{tab:pos}), tied with it on sparse npm at a week, and beaten by the dynamic count
on the grid in 23 and 28 of 30 blocks.

\paragraph{The occurrence alone, under a count law.} The fourth cell against the count hurdle differs in the
occurrence alone, the model's own $1 - F(0)$ against the Jeffreys estimate under the same shifted Poisson. On
NYC 311 the model's own occurrence is the better one, lower on the dense series by 0.03591 to 0.0467 on the
mean and on 62 to 73 of the 100. The two are closer on dense npm series and on the grid (Table~\ref{tab:pos}).
Under the Brier score, which reads the occurrence alone, the model's zero mass is favored at a week on both
panels, on 68, 62 and 54 of 100 dense npm series and 19, 23 and 19 of 30 blocks. At a month it favors the
Jeffreys estimate, with the model's zero mass lower on only 42, 43 and 38 of 100 and 15, 13 and 12 of 30. All
of this is post hoc on npm and the grid.
