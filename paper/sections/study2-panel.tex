\section{Study 2: the achieved npm panel}
\label{sec:s2panel}

The packages domain is npm. Its panel was frozen on 2026-10-15 01:57 UTC and verified at 02:00 UTC, inside
the reset readiness deadline of 2026-10-20 23:59 UTC, so the registered PyPI substitution was not invoked.
Everything in this section is computed from the context window alone, 2025-09-01 to 2026-06-02; as of
2026-10-15 02:00 UTC, when the panel was verified, no target value of 2026-06-03 to 2026-08-31 had been
opened, and the targets were first requested on 2026-10-15 at 23:15 UTC, after the run's gate
(Appendix~\ref{app:s2repro}). The frozen inventory holds 4,486,927 package names; a hash-ordered walk of
12,943 of them filled the candidate pool of 10,000 eligible packages, of which 9,998 answered the context
pull completely (Appendix~\ref{app:s2panel}). On the stratum window of 2026-03-05 to 2026-06-02 the 9,998
complete candidates fell 2,431 in the dense stratum (zero fraction below 0.5), 7,558 in the sparse stratum
(0.5 to below 0.9) and 9 in the very sparse stratum (0.9 and above). The achieved panel is therefore 209
series: 100 dense, 100 sparse and 9 very sparse, the first in selection-hash order within each stratum, with
91 very-sparse slots unfilled (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:s2panel}). The 209 names were created between
2011-08-26 and 2025-08-08; 80 of them are scoped names.

\paragraph{Why the third stratum is short.} The registered rule fixes the candidate pool at the first
10,000 eligible names in hash order and forbids a larger pool, a transfer of unused slots and any
alternative filter, so the stratum holds every complete candidate that qualified and no more. Only 9 of
9,998 complete candidates had zero downloads on at least 90 percent of the 90 stratum days. A reading, not a
measured fact: npm counts every tarball request, including mirrors and automated clients, so a published
package is rarely without a download for most of a quarter. The confirmatory family pools all 209 series as
registered, strata being secondary descriptors, and any per-stratum table shows $n = 9$ for the very sparse
stratum. The regime closest to GitHub stars, series that are zero nine days in ten, is therefore nearly
untested on npm, which is one reason the npm and GitHub typical-series results need not agree.

\paragraph{One series at 7.6 million downloads a day.} The dense stratum holds the package
\texttt{aggregate-error}, whose last 28 context days before the first cutoff average 7,637,136 downloads a
day; the next largest last-28 mean in the panel is about 13,000, and the median series averages two.
Equal series weights, as registered, mean that the mean paired difference of the unscaled \qs{} over 209
series is that one series' difference divided by 209 plus a remainder from the other 208, whose losses are
of a different order; the series-bootstrap interval inherits the same dependence. The median difference,
the win count and the signed-rank test are rank-based and not dominated by it, since the series counts once
in each; the scaled \qs{} is the registered scale-free secondary. This is a property of the registered
design, not a defect found in it, and the paper names the series wherever it prints an unscaled npm mean.
The same series is the case behind the availability rule of 2026-10-15: no discrete CRPS can be certified
for it within the registered work limit, so its CRPS is recorded as unavailable and counted in the
denominators.
