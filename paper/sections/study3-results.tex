\section{Study 3: results}
\label{sec:s3results}

The run started 2026-10-17 at 03:47 UTC and scored all 300 series in one invocation, in 939 seconds inside
the run and 956 for the whole process against a budget of 3,600 and an estimate of 1,080, with no failed
window and no resume: 39,600 forecasts (300 series, 3 origins, 2 horizons, 22 variants), every one of them
recomputed from the saved deciles, targets and laws with 0 disagreements (Appendix~\ref{app:s3tables}).
Table~\ref{tab:s3family} gives the family; Figure~\ref{fig:s3family} draws it. Twenty-one of the
thirty-six tests reject at $q = 0.05$ after the one adjustment: six are T1 tests in the dense stratum,
against \emph{hurdle\_dynamic} as H3 predicts; one is a T1 test in the sparse stratum, for
\emph{hurdle\_dynamic} as H3 predicts; and fourteen are T2 tests, all for \emph{hurdle\_dynamic}. No test
rejects in a direction opposite to H3's prediction, and no T1 median has the opposite sign. The direction of
every test is read from the paired table, the median's sign and the rank sums of the absolute nonzero
differences, which reproduce every raw $p$ exactly (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:s3direction}); a median of
exactly zero has no sign and is reported as neither direction.

\paragraph{Dense series: the predicted sign holds, and all six tests reject.} For all three models at both
horizons the substitution raises \qs{}: \emph{hurdle\_dynamic} scores higher than the completed control on 55
to 72 of the 100 series, the medians run from 0.004074 to 0.0127 and the means from 0.01511 to 0.02219,
about one percent of the control's mean score of 1.703 to 1.833, every interval lies above zero, the rank
sums favor the control in every row, and the exact ties number 0 to 2. The occurrence Brier score, declared
secondary, reads the same way here: the dynamic occurrence's Brier score is higher than the model's own
zero mass's in all six rows, by 0.00668 to 0.009347 on the mean with every interval above zero (Appendix
Table~\ref{tab:s3brier}). The accounting of the T1 difference by stratum (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:s3parts})
puts the dense loss on the zero days themselves, where the dynamic occurrence loses 0.009693 to 0.03213 in
every row, unlike the npm trade of Table~\ref{tab:s2parts}, where the substitution gained on zero days and
lost more on positive ones.

\paragraph{Sparse series: the predicted sign is not established.} One test rejects, in the predicted
direction: R21, TimesFM 2.5 at horizon 7, with a median of -0.002116, 57 series lower, 8 tied and 35 higher,
and adjusted $p$ 0.00445. One more median is negative without rejecting, R23, TimesFM 2.5 at horizon 30,
with a median of -0.0004938, 53 lower, 11 tied and 36 higher, and adjusted $p$ 0.0547. The other four
medians are exactly zero, with 12 to 23 series tied exactly, and none rejects; their rank sums lean to
\emph{hurdle\_dynamic} for R13, R15 and R17 and to the control for R19. The means run from -0.004921 to
0.002242 against control means of 0.3541 to 0.3668.

\paragraph{Very sparse series: no test rejects, and every median is exactly zero.} In each of the six
rows 72 to 88 of the 100 series score exactly the same under \emph{hurdle\_dynamic} as under the control.
The means are positive, from 0.0005086 to 0.002911, every interval includes zero, the smallest adjusted $p$
is 0.0547 (R25), and the rank sums lean to the control at horizon 7 (R25, R29, R33) and to
\emph{hurdle\_dynamic} at horizon 30 (R27, R31, R35). One series, \texttt{Sewer Maintenance} in community
board \texttt{09 QUEENS}, moves the mean most in all six rows. The ties arise from the score itself: \qs{}
reads nine deciles of a count law, which are integers, so two forecasts whose deciles coincide in every
window score identically whatever the target is, and a difference in occurrence probability smaller than one
decile is invisible to it. In these six rows 71 to 87 of the tied series have every decile at zero under both
forecasts at every origin, the completed control's deciles being all zero in every window for 71 to 95 of
the 100 very sparse series and \emph{hurdle\_dynamic}'s for 89; in the sparse rows, where 8 to 23 series
tie, 2 to 5 of them have every decile at zero under both forecasts at every origin and the rest tie with
integer deciles that are not all zero. The Brier score, declared secondary,
separates the two forecasts where the quantile score cannot: on the very sparse series the dynamic occurrence
has the lower Brier score on 68 to 82 of the 100 series in every T1 row, with medians from -0.002363 to
-0.001625 and raw $p$ at most 0.00165, while its mean differences stay within 0.0013 of zero with every
interval including zero; on the sparse series the Brier reading is mixed (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:s3brier}).
That reading is secondary and adds no test.

\paragraph{Against the empirical hurdle (T2).} \emph{hurdle\_dynamic} is lower than
\texttt{hurdle\_empirical\_28} wherever a T2 test rejects, and 14 of the 18 T2 tests reject
(Table~\ref{tab:s3t2}). In the dense stratum all six rows reject, with \emph{hurdle\_dynamic} lower on 72 to
87 of the 100 series and medians from -0.05608 to -0.06753; in the sparse stratum five of six reject (R16,
R18, R20, R22, R24), lower on 53 to 58 series with medians from -0.001852 to -0.002646, and R14, Chronos-2
at horizon 7, has a median of exactly zero and adjusted $p$ 0.138; in the very sparse stratum the three
horizon-30 rows reject (R28, R32, R36), each with 22 series lower, 77 tied and 1 higher and a median of
exactly zero, so their direction comes from the rank sums, 253 against 23, and not from a median, while the
three horizon-7 rows (R26, R30, R34) do not reject, with adjusted $p$ from 0.138 to 0.235. H3 predicts
nothing for T2; the reading is that the model's positive law, carried with the dynamic occurrence, keeps its
value on this domain against a count law whose positive law can fit a dense series, except in the four rows
whose medians are exactly zero.

\paragraph{Secondary: the fixed hurdle, the fourth cell and the pooled tables.} Everything in this paragraph
is declared secondary, unadjusted, and confirms nothing (Appendix Tables~\ref{tab:s3variants}
and~\ref{tab:s3pooled}). Study 2's hurdle with the fixed 28-day occurrence scores higher than the completed
control on the dense series for all three models at both horizons, on 60 to 70 of the 100 series; on the
sparse series it is mixed, higher on 45 to 60 of 100; and on the very sparse series it is higher in all six
rows, on 20 to 23 series against 5 to 9 lower with 69 to 73 tied and raw $p$ from 0.00293 to 0.0254, where
\emph{hurdle\_dynamic}'s six rows do not reject. The fourth cell, the model's own occurrence with the
shifted-Poisson positive law, is far worse than the control on the dense series, with mean differences of
0.2944 to 0.325 and higher on 70 to 91 of the 100, and within 0.004 of the control's mean on the sparse and
very sparse series, with 29 to 98 of the 100 series tied exactly; against \hc{}, which differs from it in
the occurrence alone, it is lower on the dense series by 0.03591 to 0.0467 on the mean and on 62 to 73 of
the 100. Pooled over all 300 series in Study 2's form, the fixed hurdle's mean is higher than its completed
control's in all six rows with more series higher than lower, lower than \hc{}'s in all six with more series
lower than higher, and each raw model's mean is lower than \lm{}'s in all six while the series split close
to evenly, 128 to 161 lower against 124 to 157 higher; every row's within-table adjusted $p$ is below 0.005.
Pooling weights the three strata equally and does not test H3.

\paragraph{Read together.} On NYC 311 the half of H3 that concerns dense series is confirmed for every
model and horizon, and the half that concerns sparse and very sparse series is not: of its twelve tests one
rejects in the predicted direction and eleven do not, ten of the twelve medians are exactly zero, and on the
very sparse series the registered score cannot see the difference that the secondary occurrence score does.
The model's positive law with the dynamic occurrence beats the empirical count hurdle in 14 of 18 tests.
Null, adverse, mixed and imprecise outcomes are reported with the same care as a positive one.
