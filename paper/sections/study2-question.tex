\section{Study 2: the question}
\label{sec:s2question}

Study 2 was registered on 2026-09-14 as a dated addition to the protocol, at commit
\texttt{df55867c982dfe2bb6e1152ec8ae93e3cd228c45} (full protocol SHA-256 at registration
\texttt{33cefd8e34fea973e6747d1ba3fe565051e215c5d1b7f6553b266c1be7a66360}), after Study 1's results were
seen, to be tested on data declared separately from Study 1's. It asks two questions about a frozen foundation
model's forecast of a daily count. First, whether replacing the model's own probability of a zero day with a
context-only occurrence probability, while keeping the model's completed positive conditional distribution,
lowers \qs{}. Second, whether that positive component, the model's own law for the size of a positive day,
improves on a fixed count hurdle whose positive law is a shifted Poisson fitted to the last 28 context days.
The registered text allows that a lower loss on zero days may coexist with a higher loss on positive days,
and says that the net sign, the typical-series sign and a null outcome are all reportable. Nothing in Study
2 amends Study 1's sample, forecasts, scores, comparisons or conclusions; the completion of a model's
deciles into a count distribution is a Study 2 assumption and is not attributed to Study 1.

The question follows from the Study 1 accounting of Section~\ref{sec:results} and the post-hoc parts of
Table~\ref{tab:posthoc}. A daily count forecast has two parts, whether anything happens and how much
happens when it does, and the foundation models supply neither part on its own terms: their native output
is nine quantiles of a continuous forecast, clipped at zero, and a zero day enters the score only through
whichever deciles the model placed at or below zero. In Study 1 the models' mean gain against \lm{} sat on
positive days where the local mean overpredicts, while the zero-day part of the paired difference was
positive, against the models, in every cell: small beside the mean gain, from 0.004 to 0.015, but in every
cell larger than that cell's median difference, which ran from 0.0003 to 0.0041. The typical repository
favored the local mean, with the models lower on only 46 to 89 of 300 repositories, and the model losses
clustered at high context zero fractions (Figure~\ref{fig:zerofraction}). A reading made after those
results, and registered as a separate study rather than tested on Study 1's run, is that the models' size
forecasts carry the gain and their occurrence forecasts carry the loss, so that an occurrence probability
estimated from the context alone, substituted for the model's zero mass, would lower the loss where the
models lose and keep it where they win. Study 2 tests that reading on data declared for the purpose: the
occurrence estimate is the 28-day Jeffreys rule, the positive law is the model's own completed law, the
control is the same completed law with its own zero mass, and the matched comparators are count hurdles
with the same occurrence rule (Section~\ref{sec:s2design}). Whether the substitution helps is the question,
not the premise.

A literature re-check for work since 2026-09-13, run on 2026-10-12 and recorded in the repository, sets
what this paper may claim and what it may not. The construction is not new in its parts, and the paper
does not claim that it is. Frozen foundation models have been evaluated on sparse daily counts: twelve of
them, the three used here among them, on five sparse panels as an event-ranking task without a proper
score, with the occurrence-size output head named as the next step \citep{schoess2026}. An
occurrence-times-size hurdle has been applied to frozen Chronos-2 output, on monthly spare-parts demand,
with the occurrence probability from the full history and the result judged by point accuracy and service
measures, where it trailed plain re-centring \citep{chin2026}. TimesFM 3's nine-decile pinball loss on
simulated intermittent counts has been reported as no better than the history's empirical deciles
\citep{ebrahim2026}. That aggregate and per-series rankings of zero-shot models can disagree has been shown
on a marketplace panel and on M5 \citep{islam2026} and on quick-commerce demand \citep{kim2026}. A later
test window on familiar entities has been shown not to establish generalization to unseen ones
\citep{moghadasi2026}, and a live benchmark scores daily new stargazers on the 400 most-starred
repositories \citep{garza2026}. Earlier items in the protocol's lineage bear on the same ground: a
large-language-model study of intermittent demand on an industrial dataset \citep{tsantilis2025}, a
zero-inflated dual-head forecaster \citep{zodiac2026}, the fev-bench practice of reporting win rates beside
relative errors \citep{fevbench2026}, and a study of information leakage in the evaluation of time-series
foundation models \citep{meyer2025}.
Hurdle-count forecasting without foundation models has current reference implementations
\citep{damato2026, bai2026}, and one zero-shot foundation model built for frequent zeros was released in the
window, with a quantile head and no count head; it was not evaluated here \citep{exaone2026}.

What the re-check did not find, as of 2026-10-12, is a published evaluation in which a context-only
occurrence probability replaces the zero mass of frozen Chronos-2 or TimesFM forecasts while their completed
positive conditional distribution is kept, judged by a proper quantile score against a completed-unwrapped
control, matched count hurdles and a dynamic count baseline on sparse daily counts. ``Not found'' means
those searches did not find it, not that it does not exist; two of the routes failed and papers under
review are not visible by any route used. The paper therefore claims the registered combination and nothing
broader: the completed-unwrapped control, the short-window occurrence estimate, the matched hurdle
comparators, the proper score, the probability sample and the side-by-side mean and typical-series
summaries, on daily attention and adoption counts. It does not claim to be the first evaluation of frozen
foundation models on sparse daily counts, the first hurdle applied to a frozen model's output, or the first
to show that aggregate and per-series rankings disagree.
