\section{The occurrence substitution, by regime}
\label{sec:occurrence}

The substitution keeps a model's completed positive law and replaces its zero mass with an occurrence
probability estimated from the context alone. Table~\ref{tab:occ} gives it against the model's own
completed forecast on every panel and stratum, under the registered \qs{} and under the two declared
secondaries, with the post-hoc TSB hurdle beside it, and Figure~\ref{fig:signature} draws the same contrast
series by series against the context zero fraction.

\paragraph{The pooled answer.} On the pooled families of Study 2 the substitution does not help. Against its
completed control the fixed hurdle's mean difference is positive, favoring the control, in all six npm cells,
from 90.24 to 253.9 (carried by \texttt{aggregate-error}), with every interval above zero, and two of the six
tests reject against it, TimesFM 3 and TimesFM 2.5 at horizon 30. No pooled test favors the substitution at
$q = 0.05$; in E01 the typical series leans to the hurdle, lower on 121 of 209 series with a median of
-0.01376. On the grid all three tests reject against the hurdle, with means from 0.08599 to 0.08769, with
every interval above zero, and the hurdle lower on 5 or 6 of the 30 replicate blocks. On the mean, in every
one of the nine cells, the substitution lowers the zero-day loss and raises the positive-day underprediction
loss by more (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:s2parts}); the median npm series does not follow that trade, its
zero-day difference being positive, against the hurdle, in all six cells (0.003 to 0.036).

\paragraph{Dense series: the model's own zero mass is better.} On dense series the substitution is worse in
all three settings. On npm the fixed hurdle is worse for the typical dense series: it scores lower on 32 to 43 of 100 in every
cell. On the grid at a month, by zero probability, it beats its control in 4 to 6 of 30 replicate blocks at
$z = 0.2$ against 21 to 26 at $z = 0.95$. On NYC 311 all six registered dense tests reject as H3 predicted: the dynamic hurdle scores
higher than the completed control on 55 to 72 of the 100 series, the medians run from 0.004074 to 0.0127 and
the means from 0.01511 to 0.02219, with every interval above zero, and the dynamic occurrence's Brier score
is higher than the model's own zero mass's in all six rows, by 0.00668 to 0.009347 on the mean with every
interval above zero.  On
npm the Brier score does not follow the quantile score at a month: the fixed hurdle's Brier score is lower
than the control's on 57 to 62 of the 100 dense series at horizon 30 while \qs{} still favors the control,
where at horizon 7 it is lower on only 32 to 46; Appendix~\ref{app:s3tables} returns to that. The TSB hurdle, post hoc, is
far behind every model on dense series (Table~\ref{tab:occ}).

\paragraph{Sparse series: the answer depends on the domain.} On the sparse npm stratum the substitution helps
the typical series at a week under all three scores: against its completed control the fixed hurdle scores
lower on 81, 60 and 66 of the 100 sparse series at horizon 7, for Chronos-2, TimesFM 3 and TimesFM 2.5, and
on 58, 45 and 54 of them at horizon 30; on the occurrence Brier score it is lower on 85, 64 and 70 at horizon
7 and on the discrete CRPS on 82, 70 and 70; and a post-hoc signed-rank test within the stratum has raw $p$
below 0.002 for all three sparse-stratum contrasts at horizon 7 and of 0.47 or more at horizon 30, so the
test is null at a month under \qs{} and the CRPS, though Chronos-2's hurdle still wins the typical series
there (Table~\ref{tab:occ}). The TSB hurdle does the same at a
week, lower than the completed controls on 77, 61 and 74 of 100 and level with the count hurdle, 49 lower
against 49 higher. It is a secondary reading on one domain and one horizon, and it did not reproduce on NYC
311. There one test
rejects, in the predicted direction: R21, TimesFM 2.5 at horizon 7, with a median of -0.002116, 57 series
lower, 8 tied and 35 higher, and adjusted $p$ 0.00445; R23 leans the same way without rejecting; the other
four medians are exactly zero, with 12 to 23 series tied exactly, and none rejects; and the Brier reading is
mixed. Study 2's own fixed hurdle, scored on NYC 311 (Appendix~\ref{app:s3tables}), scores lower on 41, 39
and 43 of the 100 at horizon 7 against 81, 60 and 66 on npm. So on sparse NYC 311 series the score sees the substitution, and the npm reading did not carry over.

\paragraph{Very sparse series: an interface limit.} On NYC 311's very sparse stratum no T1 test rejects and
every median is exactly zero: 72 to 88 of the 100 series score exactly the same under the dynamic hurdle as
under the control in each row, the means are positive, from 0.0005086 to 0.002911, every interval includes
zero, and one series, \texttt{Sewer Maintenance} in community board \texttt{09 QUEENS}, moves each mean most.
The Brier score separates the two forecasts where the quantile score cannot: the dynamic occurrence has the
lower Brier score on 68 to 82 of the 100 series in every T1 row, with medians from -0.002363 to -0.001625 and
raw $p$ at most 0.00165, while its mean differences stay within 0.0013 of zero. What the ties and the
separation compare is the completion rule, not the model. Counted after the run from the saved deciles and
descriptive: 71 to 87 of the tied series in each row have every decile at zero under both forecasts in every
window. So on series that are zero nine days in ten the registered construction cannot ask the
occurrence question: the quantile score ties, and the Brier score's separation is the dynamic hurdle's
estimate against a constant near 0.061, which the formula predicts. The same holds for the TSB hurdle
(Table~\ref{tab:occ}, Figure~\ref{fig:signature}) and not for the grid's cells with $z = 0.95$ at a month,
where the substitution wins under all three scores (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:s2cells}). The limit belongs to
the models' output and to the completion as much as to the score.
