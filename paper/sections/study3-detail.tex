\section{Study 3: the design and the panel, the remaining detail}
\label{app:s3detail}

\paragraph{Registration.} Study 3 was registered on 2026-10-16 as item 7 of the protocol's dated additions,
at commit \texttt{3357d779db28cffa4048e7e333fd02b9434b4b6d} (full protocol SHA-256 with the addition
\texttt{05c7617b0391cbcca7bccddb6ff54a763d47ba88e5d31dac4bd98621a9721b29}), appended on top of commit
\texttt{04e92c1} after Study 2's results had been seen and before any Study 3 target value was accessed; the
only Study 3 source material in hand was the NYC 311 cell inventory of 2024, captured on 2026-09-14, which
holds no target-period count. Its hypothesis H3 is stated in the addition: on series whose context zero
fraction is at least 0.5, the substitution with a dynamic occurrence lowers \qs{} relative to the model's
own completed forecast; on series below 0.5 it raises it. Each stratum is tested on its own; nothing is
pooled across strata for confirmation; null, adverse, mixed and imprecise results receive the same reporting.
Where the addition says ``as registered'', Study 2's registration at \texttt{df55867} applies. Study 3
changes four things from Study 2's construction, each prompted by a Study 2 result: the tests are per
stratum, so that the confirmatory answer speaks for the regime it is asked about and not for whatever
dominates a pool; the occurrence probability is the registered \texttt{dynamic\_count} law's discounted
estimate rather than the fixed 28-day Jeffreys estimate, which ignores persistence; the fourth cell of the
occurrence-by-size factorial is run as a declared secondary; and the primary count comparator is the
empirical hurdle, whose positive law can fit a dense series. What Study 3 does not answer follows from what
the registration covers: it tests no Markov occurrence estimate, adds no overdispersed cell to the grid,
says nothing about Wikipedia pageviews, and leaves the npm very sparse stratum, with its nine series, as
Study 2 left it.

\paragraph{Domain, frame and strata.} The series key is the problem type by community board, enumerated
from the frozen 2024 inventory of 9,496 cells captured on 2026-09-14, of which 8,469 carry a recognized
board and problem and form the whole eligible frame, with no candidate pool. Counts are requests by
creation date in New York calendar days with daylight saving time, deduplicated by request identifier with
the latest captured revision kept, counting requests and not closures. The strata are Study 2's, computed
on the context zero fraction of 2026-03-05 through 2026-06-02: $[0, 0.5)$ dense, $[0.5, 0.9)$ sparse,
$[0.9, 1]$ very sparse; with selection seed 20261001 the first 100 complete series of each stratum in
selection-hash order are taken, with no transfer of slots. The context from 2025-09-01 through 2026-06-02
is inspected for completeness and the strata only. A request is a complaint or inquiry about a city
service, a third kind of daily count beside GitHub stars and npm downloads.

\paragraph{Calendar, models and variants.} The calendar is Study 2's: context from 2025-09-01, cutoffs
2026-06-03, 2026-07-03 and 2026-08-02 with 275, 305 and 335 context days, horizons 7 and 30, endpoint
2026-08-31, in New York calendar days. The three foundation models are the Study 1 pins, with Study 2's
completion, scores and baselines unchanged, and all 22 registered variants are forecast at every origin.
For each model four variants are scored from the same saved base forecast with no refitting:
\emph{completed}, the control; \emph{hurdle\_fixed}, Study 2's hurdle with the 28-day Jeffreys occurrence;
\emph{hurdle\_dynamic}, new, which takes at each lead the occurrence probability of the registered
\texttt{dynamic\_count} law, a discounted Beta-Bernoulli with discount 0.98 started at $a_B = b_B = 0.5$, with
the model's completed positive law; and \emph{occurrence\_model\_size\_count}, new, the fourth factorial
cell, the model's own completed occurrence $1 - F(0)$ with \hc{}'s positive law. No variant is fitted or
selected on targets.

\paragraph{The confirmatory family.} For each stratum, model and horizon, T1 compares the model's
\emph{hurdle\_dynamic} with its completed control and T2 compares it with \texttt{hurdle\_empirical\_28}:
$3 \times 3 \times 2 \times 2 = 36$ \qs{} tests, R01 to R36, listed in Table~\ref{tab:s3structure} and
committed before the gate. Differences are variant minus reference, so negative favors the variant. H3
predicts a positive T1 median in the dense stratum and a negative one in the sparse and very sparse strata,
and nothing for T2; the tests are two-sided as registered, with the direction read from the median and
reported beside the mean. Within each stratum origins are averaged within series and horizon, the 100 series
weigh equally and are the pairing and bootstrap units (20,000 replicates, pointwise 95 percent percentile
intervals, bootstrap seed 20261004), the two-sided approximate Wilcoxon signed-rank test omits zero
differences and reports $p = 1$ when every difference is zero, and Benjamini-Hochberg is applied once at
$q = 0.05$ over the 36. A stratum with fewer than ten complete series would have made its twelve tests
unavailable, with the family not shrunk by any outcome, and the BH correction then covers the available
tests; none was. Everything else, the fixed hurdle and the fourth cell against the control and the count
hurdles per stratum, the pooled 300-series tables in Study 2's form, the scaled score, the Brier score, the
CRPS, the PIT, the accounting and the concentration descriptors, is declared secondary and adds no test.

\paragraph{The census and the strata.} The counts come from NYC Open Data's dataset \texttt{erm2-nwe9} by
one anonymous query per New York calendar day, each exhausted on one page. The context pull of 2026-10-16
made 276 attempts, 275 census days and one metadata read, in two invocations over 1,124 seconds within an
approval of 330 attempts and 3 hours, and captured 2,959,469 requests, 2,902,433 of them in frame cells,
with no source gap. The panel was frozen and verified on 2026-10-16 at 08:04 UTC, inside the registered
deadline of 2026-10-27, with no target value opened. On the stratum window the 8,469 frame cells fell 2,002
dense, 2,422 sparse and 4,045 very sparse, and each stratum took its first 100 complete series in
selection-hash order, so the panel is the registered 300 with no unfilled slot. Of the very sparse stratum's
100 series, 39 have no request in the 90 stratum days and 19 none in the whole 275-day context at the first
origin; no dense or sparse series has either. At the first origin 74 of the 100 very sparse series and 4 of
the 100 sparse series have fewer than two request days in the last 28 context days, where
\emph{hurdle\_dynamic} equals the fixed hurdle by the registered fallback (Table~\ref{tab:s3context}).

\paragraph{The targets.} The 90 target days, 2026-06-03 to 2026-08-31, were counted on 2026-10-17 by the
same census under the signed gate, in 106 attempts of an approved 120 over two invocations: 100 attempts one
second apart exhausted 84 days, 16 of them repeats after the server throttled or failed a request, and 6
attempts three seconds apart exhausted the last 6 days at the first attempt each. The census found 983,482
requests, 947,751 in frame cells, 18,660 with a missing or unrecognized board or problem and 17,071 in
recognized cells outside the 2024 frame; no day was a source gap, the fewest frame requests on any day being
8,849 on 2026-08-16. A separate recomputation from the raw captures, sharing no code with the pipeline,
reproduced the census totals, every series' 90 target counts and the unchanged context. The targets were
read 47 days after the last target day, with no check for later revision of the source. The dataset's
metadata states no license and links no terms page; the city website's terms of use, read on 2026-10-17,
govern the site, allow lawful use only, reserve the City's rights in its materials, give no warranty and
state that links to other sites imply no endorsement, and say nothing specific about datasets, their license
or attribution; the Open Data Law that the program names as its basis was not read. The contamination
ledger keeps Study 2's three categories for NYC 311 unchanged: no exact source or entity overlap is
established in the reviewed disclosures; civic data may occur in broadly described training corpora, and
absence of disclosure is not absence from training; and the retrospective calendar does not certify that the
2026 target days postdate the availability of all three checkpoints.

\paragraph{The dynamic occurrence and its fallback.} The registered \texttt{dynamic\_count} law keeps four
parameters that start at $0.5$, are multiplied by $0.98$ before each context observation and updated by it;
its occurrence probability at a lead is $a_B / (a_B + b_B)$ from the Beta-Bernoulli pair. \emph{hurdle\_dynamic}
takes that probability as its zero mass, $H(0) = 1 - p_{\text{dyn}}$, with the model's completed positive
conditional law $G_+$ above it, exactly as Study 2's hurdle uses the Jeffreys $p$; the two differ in the
occurrence alone. With fewer than two request days in the last 28 days of a context, \texttt{dynamic\_count}
takes \hc{}'s law by its registered rule, and \emph{hurdle\_dynamic} then equals \emph{hurdle\_fixed} for that
window. Table~\ref{tab:s3context} counts the series concerned at each origin, from the signals, before and
apart from any score: no dense series at any origin, 4 to 8 of the 100 sparse series, and 72 to 74 of the
100 very sparse series, of which 54 to 58 have no request day in the last 28 and 15 to 19 none in the whole
context.

\paragraph{Completeness, the source gap and the fallback domain.} For an event-enumerated source a day is
complete when the capture of that day's requests is exhausted; a cell-day absent from an exhausted
enumeration has zero requests; a calendar day with zero requests across the whole enumerated frame is a
source gap, not a zero, and stops the domain before any scoring. PyPI through ClickPy, under Study 2's
registered PyPI rules with the same strata and seeds, was the sole fallback, to be used only if NYC 311
could not provide a verified achieved panel by 2026-10-27 23:59 UTC, with the operational reason recorded
before any target scoring and never unfavorable performance or an unfilled stratum; had both failed, Study 3
would have been reported unavailable. Neither clause was reached.

\paragraph{Seeds, gate and smoke.} The seeds are selection 20261001, forecasts 20261002, PIT 20261003 and
bootstrap 20261004, derived as in Study 2's inference command with the component name \texttt{study3}.
Study 2's gate requirements apply unchanged: a signed readiness bound to the addition's commit, a GPU smoke
at the implementation commit, a clean committed tree, separate target-access and runtime approvals, the
committed comparison file, checkpoints with hashes, the committed inference command with its per-row
recomputation, the capture ledger, and the source's terms recorded before publication. The implementation
commit is \texttt{00fd91dccfc86b1e4bbd2f0cb44a47052937458d}; the smoke passed there on 2026-10-17 at 01:56
UTC with a peak reserved memory of 2,948,595,712 bytes; the readiness was approved at 03:12 UTC with a
runtime budget of 3,600 seconds against an estimate of 1,080; and the gate passed three times, at 03:15 UTC
before the first target request, at 03:44 UTC before the pull resumed, and at 03:46 UTC before the run, with
the pull's signals admitted only together with their completeness document. The pull and run commands run
the gate again themselves before any access. Study 3 adds no test to any Study 2 family and changes nothing
above its heading in the protocol.

\paragraph{The target pull.} The first invocation ran from 03:16 to 03:32 UTC on 2026-10-17, one second
between attempts: 100 attempts, 84 days, stopping at the approved ceiling as the client is built to; since
every day was answered on one page, the 16 extra attempts were repeats after the server answered 429 or a
5xx status and the client waited, and the client does not record which. The second invocation, approved
with 20 more attempts and a three-second spacing, ran from 03:45:06 to 03:45:43 UTC: 6 attempts for the
last 6 days, each answered at the first attempt. Every capture was acquired after 2026-08-31 had ended in
New York. The signals join the 90 target days to the frozen 275-day context, which was unchanged and not
re-requested; the completeness document lists each day's census.

\paragraph{The terms and the time basis.} The dataset's metadata, read on 2026-10-16 at 07:44 UTC, names
\texttt{erm2-nwe9}, ``311 Service Requests from 2020 to Present'', owned by NYC OpenData, with empty license
fields, the attribution ``311'' and no terms link. NYC Open Data's overview page answered a scripted request
on 2026-10-17 with a redirect to the city's main website, which refused the scripted request at its new
address; two pages were then read in a desktop browser on 2026-10-17 at 04:48 and 04:49 UTC and their text
saved with its hash: the program's page, which names New York City's Open Data Law as its basis and links the
city website's terms of use, and the terms themselves, as Section~\ref{sec:limitations} summarizes them. The
field \texttt{created\_date} is a floating timestamp with no zone and no stated local time; the captures
settle it: on the spring-forward Sunday, 2026-03-08, the hour from 02:00 holds 1 request against 197 and 225
on the Sundays either side, on the fall-back Sunday, 2025-11-02, the hour from 01:00 holds 796 against 528
and 339, and the hour with the fewest requests falls between 03:00 and 05:59 on 251 of the 275 context days.
The values behave as New York wall-clock time with daylight saving time, which the census assumes.

\paragraph{Data statement.} The daily counts of the 300 series were obtained from NYC Open Data's SODA
endpoint for dataset \texttt{erm2-nwe9}, \url{https://data.cityofnewyork.us/resource/erm2-nwe9.json},
anonymously, with no application token and no credential: the context of 2025-09-01 to 2026-06-02 on
2026-10-16 and the targets of 2026-06-03 to 2026-08-31 on 2026-10-17 under a separate target-access
approval (Appendix~\ref{app:s3tables}). A request is a 311 service request as the City records it, counted
on its creation day and never on its closure; it is a complaint or inquiry about a city service, not a
measure of the underlying condition. The records name the source as NYC Open Data and claim no endorsement
by the City; the dataset's description states that it reveals no personally identifying information about
the requester, and no field beyond the identifier, the creation timestamp, the problem type and the board
was kept. The frozen frame, the selection, the census of context counts, the status file, the run's
inference and integrity outputs, the results page and its provenance are in the repository; the 300
whole-series checkpoints (645 MB), the raw captures and the target signals are not, and the checkpoints are
to be published as a release asset. The project's compilation is released under CC BY 4.0; no license is
asserted over the counts as such.
