\paragraph{The Study 2 runs.} The synthetic run started 2026-10-15 20:16 UTC at implementation commit
\texttt{3fc3411f7c683aaf98e6fad28b26aa9c92ab00b9}, after the signed readiness (approved 20:13 UTC, runtime
budget 2,400 seconds against an estimate of 1,539) and the gate, which passed against the signed readiness,
the GPU smoke at the implementation commit and the committed grid manifest, whose generated copy equals it;
it completed its 540 series in one invocation in 1,317 seconds (1,323 for the whole process) with 55,080
forecasts (540 series, 3 origins, 2 horizons, the 16 variants and the oracle), no failed window and no
resume. The npm run started 2026-10-15 23:27 UTC at implementation commit
\texttt{3057b16dbd2f9b218449352717ef4adabc2f72f7}, after the signed readiness (approved 23:09 UTC, at most
300 target requests, a budget of 3,600 seconds against an estimate of 1,741) and the gate, which passed at
23:14 UTC before any target request and again at 23:26 UTC with the pulled signals and their completeness
document; it completed its 209 series in one invocation in 692 seconds (699 for the whole process) with
20,064 forecasts (209 series, 3 origins, 2 horizons, 16 variants), no failed window and no resume. Both ran
on the same NVIDIA GeForce RTX 4070 SUPER (driver 610.88, CUDA 12.8, PyTorch 2.11.0) at batch size one,
with a peak reserved memory of 2,948,595,712 bytes, from the locally cached weights. The targets, the 209
series' download ranges for 2026-06-03 to 2026-08-31, were requested once each from the download-counts API
between 23:15 and 23:26 UTC, 209 attempts and 209 answers, with no retry and no failure; every answer lists
each of the 90 days once with a nonnegative integer. The integrity recomputation of the npm run verified all
209 checkpoint hashes and recomputed \qs{}, its three accounting parts, the context-scaled \qs{} and, where
a count law is saved, the occurrence Brier score for all 20,064 forecasts from the saved deciles, targets
and laws, with 0 disagreements; the recomputation of the synthetic run verified all 540 checkpoint hashes
and recomputed \qs{}, its parts and the scaled score for all 55,080 forecasts with a largest absolute
difference of 0.0, and found every saved target and context mean equal to the registered generator's
series. The discrete CRPS was recorded as unavailable, under the addition of 2026-10-15, for 66 npm
forecasts, all of them on \texttt{aggregate-error} (11 variants with a count law, 3 origins, 2 horizons);
the other 13,728 forecasts with a count law are certified, and the 6,270 without one (the raw deciles,
\lm{} and \aets{}) have no CRPS by construction. On the synthetic grid every forecast with a count law is
certified. The inference of both runs ran at a HEAD that differs from the implementation commit only under
the documentation directory, with no uncommitted change under the scripts or the package. The zero-day
sensitivity ran on 2026-10-16 at HEAD \texttt{e41fdf2} and the dynamic-occurrence analysis on 2026-10-16
from 05:29 to 05:37 UTC at HEAD \texttt{ff8c5e8}, each with no uncommitted change under the inference
script or the package. NumPy 2.5.3, SciPy 1.18.1 and pandas 2.3.3 were used throughout; each provenance
file records this. The 540 synthetic checkpoints (about 0.9 GB) and the 209 npm checkpoints (338 MB), one
JSON file per series with every forecast, are not in the tree; their SHA-256 values are in each run's state
file.

\paragraph{The post-hoc intermittent-demand benchmark.} The TSB hurdle, its two sensitivities at smoothing
weights 0.05 and 0.2, the fourth factorial cell on npm and the grid, and the field's point methods, Croston's
method, its bias-corrected variant and ADIDA from the pinned \texttt{statsforecast}, were scored from the
saved forecasts of all three runs on 2026-10-17 under a specification fixed before any score on the page
existed; no model was run and nothing was refitted to a target. Before any output was written the command
verified every checkpoint hash, rebuilt the three registered families byte for byte from the saved scores,
rebuilt every context from the frozen signals, and checked in every window that the TSB level product equals
the pinned library's own TSB forecast. The point methods are scored as a point mass at the rounded forecast,
under which \qs{} and the discrete CRPS both equal the absolute error; a point mass loses to every forecast
with a law by construction, and the benchmark says nothing about those methods as point forecasts. Its
results page, under \texttt{reports/study2-posthoc-intermittent-baselines/}, gives every contrast with its
$n$ and no verdict.

\paragraph{Commands.} Place the release assets, the checkpoints, in the run directories, then from the
repository root with the project's Python environment (PowerShell; a completed run directory is immutable,
so the inference, integrity, sensitivity and post-hoc commands write to a new directory):
{\footnotesize
\begin{verbatim}
.\.venv\Scripts\python.exe scripts\study2_inference.py synthetic `
    --run reports\study2-synthetic-2026-10-15 --out <new directory>
.\.venv\Scripts\python.exe reports\study2-synthetic-2026-10-15\recompute_scores.py `
    reports\study2-synthetic-2026-10-15 --out <new file>
.\.venv\Scripts\python.exe reports\study2-synthetic-2026-10-15\make_tables.py `
    reports\study2-synthetic-2026-10-15\inference
.\.venv\Scripts\python.exe scripts\study2_inference.py external_packages `
    --run reports\study2-npm\run-2026-10-15 --out <new directory>
.\.venv\Scripts\python.exe scripts\study2_integrity.py external_packages `
    --run reports\study2-npm\run-2026-10-15 --out <new directory>
.\.venv\Scripts\python.exe reports\study2-npm\run-2026-10-15\make_tables.py `
    reports\study2-npm\run-2026-10-15\inference
.\.venv\Scripts\python.exe reports\study2-npm\run-2026-10-15\check_directions.py `
    reports\study2-npm\run-2026-10-15\inference
.\.venv\Scripts\python.exe reports\study2-npm\run-2026-10-15\posthoc\sensitivity_zero_days.py `
    reports\study2-npm\run-2026-10-15 --out <new directory>
.\.venv\Scripts\python.exe reports\study2-posthoc-dynamic-occurrence\dynamic_occurrence.py `
    --npm reports\study2-npm\run-2026-10-15 --synthetic reports\study2-synthetic-2026-10-15 `
    --out <new directory>
.\.venv\Scripts\python.exe reports\study2-posthoc-intermittent-baselines\intermittent_baselines.py `
    --out <new directory>
.\.venv\Scripts\python.exe paper\figures\make_figures.py
.\.venv\Scripts\python.exe paper\check_numbers.py
\end{verbatim}}
The first regenerates the synthetic family and its secondary tables from the saved forecasts without
refitting; the second recomputes every saved synthetic score; the third prints the synthetic results
document's tables; the fourth to seventh do the same for the npm run, the integrity check recomputing every
score and verifying every checkpoint hash; the eighth re-scores the saved npm forecasts without the three
zero days; the ninth computes the dynamic-occurrence wrapper and its two post-hoc families from the saved
laws of both runs; the tenth scores the post-hoc intermittent-demand benchmark from the saved forecasts of
all three runs; the eleventh draws every figure of this paper from the saved files; the twelfth asserts
every number in this paper against the files that \texttt{paper/sources.md} names, including the post-hoc
stratum and empirical-hurdle readings, the completion-floor counts and the secondary-score contrasts, which
it recomputes from the saved per-series scores and the checkpoints. The Study 1 commands are in
Appendix~\ref{app:repro} and the Study 3 commands in Appendix~\ref{app:s3tables}. The runs themselves, the
gates, the target pulls and the panel pulls are not to be repeated: the gate and the pull pass only at the
implementation commit with the readiness files in place, the pull refuses to write a second snapshot, and a
new target request needs its own approval. A reader who recomputes a test from the CSVs should parse the
floats exactly (pandas \texttt{float\_precision="round\_trip"}); the default parser can move a last digit and
change a rank tie.
