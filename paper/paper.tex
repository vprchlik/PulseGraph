\begin{abstract}
We ask whether zero-shot time-series foundation models improve probabilistic forecasts of sparse daily
counts, and test one mechanism. Both studies are registered and population-sampled. Study 1 (protocol
commit \texttt{5f01769}, 2026-09-12) re-evaluates Chronos-2, TimesFM 3 and TimesFM 2.5 on daily GitHub star
counts of 300 repositories sampled from 148,550, against a 28-day local mean and weekly AutoETS under a
nine-decile quantile score. The typical repository favors the local mean: the models score lower on 46 to
89 of 300 and the signed-rank test rejects on the reference's side in all nine cells, while the equal-weight
mean favors the models in every cell, carried by few repositories; against AutoETS both summaries favor the
models. Study 2 (commit \texttt{df55867}, 2026-09-14) asks whether replacing a frozen model's zero mass with
a 28-day context-only occurrence probability, keeping its completed positive law, lowers the score, on an
npm download panel of 209 hash-sampled series and a registered 540-series synthetic grid; two registered
domains are deferred. No pooled test favors the substitution at $q = 0.05$, and two of six npm tests and
all three grid tests reject against it. The answer depends on the regime: in the declared secondary strata
the substitution scores lower for 81, 60 and 66 of the 100 sparse series at horizon 7 and for 32 to 43 of
the 100 dense ones, so the pooled families speak for the dense regime. The model's positive law beats a
shifted-Poisson count hurdle in all six npm cells, and the registered dynamic count beats every model on
the grid. Every npm mean is carried by one series at 7.6 million downloads a day; the medians, counts and
rank tests describe the typical series. The claim is the registered combination on npm downloads and the
grid, and nothing broader.
\end{abstract}

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{What the two studies say together.} Study 1's post-hoc accounting located the models' mean gain
against the local mean on positive days where the local mean overpredicts, and a zero-day part against the
models in every cell, small beside the mean gain but larger than every median difference. Study 2 asked
whether that zero-day loss can be taken out by a context-only occurrence estimate while the model's
completed positive law is kept. On the pooled registered families it cannot be taken out without paying
more on positive days: on the mean, in all nine hurdle-against-control cells the substitution lowers the
zero-day loss and raises the positive-day underprediction loss by more (Table~\ref{tab:s2parts}), two of six
npm tests and all three synthetic tests reject against it, and no test favors it at $q = 0.05$. The pooled
answer is the dense regime's answer: the 209 series weigh equally on an unscaled score and the 18 cells
weigh equally, so the dense stratum and the cells with few zeros and large positive means decide the
families, and there a hurdle that forces 1.7 percent of zero mass onto a series with no recent zero must
lose. In the regime the question was asked about, the declared secondary strata say the opposite at short
horizons: the typical sparse npm series favors the substitution at horizon 7 for all three models, the strata
are null at 30 days, and on the grid the hurdle beats its control in 21 to 26 of 30 blocks at $z = 0.95$
against 4 to 6 at $z = 0.2$. The occurrence Brier score, where it is lower for the hurdle (E01, E04, E13),
does not carry over to the mean quantile score, which \texttt{aggregate-error} carries; in those three rows
the median and the count lean to the hurdle, none significantly. The sentence the paper stands behind is
therefore conditional: a context-only occurrence estimate does not improve a frozen model's own completed
zero mass under this score on the pooled npm panel or the pooled grid, hurts the typical dense series, and
helps the typical sparse series at a week's horizon; a dynamic occurrence estimate narrows the gap and does
not close it; and a registered test of the regime dependence on a second domain is the next study.

\paragraph{Two readings of the accounting trade.} The hurdle's positive law $G_+$ is the model's completed
marginal law truncated at one, not a size forecast the model produced for positive days. A model that
handles zero days by pulling its whole quantile grid down, which is what nine continuous deciles clipped at
zero must do, leaves a truncated law that sits low on positive days; adding context zero mass on top of it
counts the zeros twice, and zero-day loss down with underprediction up by more is exactly what that
predicts. The first reading of the net sign, that the context-only occurrence estimate is worse than the
model's own zero mass, and the second, that the truncated law is not the model's size forecast so that any
added zero mass hurts, are both consistent with every number here. The registered design ran three of the
four cells of the occurrence-by-size factorial and not the fourth, the model's own occurrence with a count
positive law, which is computable from the saved laws and would separate the two; it is left untested. The
premise is also weaker than Section~\ref{sec:s2question} might suggest: on the occurrence Brier score the
models' own zero mass is about as good as the Jeffreys estimate on both panels (Appendix
Tables~\ref{tab:s2scores} and~\ref{tab:s2synscores}).

\paragraph{The positive law and the dynamic count.} The second contrast isolates the positive law. On npm
the model's completed positive law beats the shifted Poisson fitted to the last 28 days in all six cells, on
the mean, the median and the rank test; but a Poisson cannot fit a series with thousands of downloads a day,
so on dense series this is a win over a comparator that cannot win, and against the registered empirical
hurdle, read post hoc, the advantage holds at 30 days and on dense series and not at horizon 7 for two of
the three models. On the grid the model's positive law loses to the shifted Poisson for two models and is
not rejected for the third, which leans the same way, where the Poisson is the generator's own law up to its
estimated mean. The registered dynamic count, a four-parameter discounted Bayesian hurdle with no foundation
model, is the best forecaster on the grid after the oracle, lower than each completed model in 18 to 24 of
30 blocks, and it loses to the completed models on 108 to 146 of 209 npm series; with the occurrence held
equal, the foundation models' positive laws underpredict the grid's positive days more than its negative
binomial does. What differs between the two settings is reported, not explained: the grid's positive law is
the count baselines' family and the npm series' is not, and the npm panel spans scales from a median of two
downloads a day to millions where the grid holds $\mu_{+}$ at 1, 5 and 25.

\paragraph{The raw contrast on a second domain.} On npm the raw deciles beat the local mean on the mean in
all six cells and reject on the rank test in four of six; in Study 1 the rank test favored the reference in
all nine cells, and on the 400 most-starred repositories of a live benchmark the typical series favors the
models \citep{garza2026}. The three designs differ in what is counted, in how the panel is drawn, in scale,
in sparsity, in horizon and in calendar; the paper reports the differences and does not attribute them. In
both studies the mean is carried by few series, and the context-scaled mean, the registered secondary and
the summary now recommended in print for intermittent data \citep{corani2026}, agrees with the medians where
the unscaled mean differs.

\paragraph{What the paper claims, and what it does not.} The paper claims the registered combination and
nothing broader: a context-only occurrence probability substituted for the zero mass of frozen Chronos-2 and
TimesFM forecasts, their completed positive law kept, judged by a proper quantile score against a
completed-unwrapped control and matched count hurdles, with a dynamic count baseline beside them, on a
hash-sampled panel of daily npm download counts and on the registered synthetic grid, with the mean and the
typical series reported side by side; Study 1's GitHub star counts enter through the raw comparison alone,
and no attention-count series went through the hurdle construction. It does not claim to be the first
evaluation of frozen foundation models on sparse daily counts \citep{schoess2026, adler2025,
ebrahim2026}, the first hurdle applied to a frozen model's output \citep{chin2026}, or the first to show
that aggregate and per-series rankings disagree \citep{islam2026, kim2026, brini2026, cherif2026}; a later
test window on familiar entities is not shown here to establish generalization to unseen ones
\citep{moghadasi2026, qiao2026}. The paper does not show that the models forecast bursts, that the
substitution would fare the same on NYC 311 requests or Wikipedia pageviews, which were registered and are
not run, that any result transfers to launches or to the days after a release, or that an npm download is a
human adoption. Study 2's answer is to the registered question as registered, on the panel it achieved, with
the source's three zero days scored as served and a post-hoc sensitivity beside them.

\section{Limitations}

Study 1's limitations stand as the report gave them; Study 2 adds its own.

\begin{itemize}
\input{shared/s1-limitations-items}
\item \emph{Three zero days in the npm targets.} The download API served 0 for all 209 series on
  2026-07-12 and 2026-08-14 and 0 for the largest series on 2026-06-25, between days of millions. The
  registered completeness rule accepted them as explicit counts, the run scored them so, and the dated
  addition of 2026-10-16 keeps that analysis as the confirmatory one; the post-hoc sensitivity changes no
  conclusion without all three days and one row, E06, without the two panel-wide days. Nothing here
  verifies that they are gaps.
\item \emph{One series carries every npm mean.} With equal series weights on the unscaled score, as
  registered, every mean in Table~\ref{tab:s2ext} is a statement about \texttt{aggregate-error}; the
  medians, counts, rank tests and per-stratum counts describe the typical series, and the context-scaled
  score is the registered scale-free secondary.
\item \emph{The very sparse stratum holds nine series, and the regime reading is secondary.} Only 9 of
  9,998 complete candidates were sparse enough for the third stratum, so the external mechanism test says
  nothing about series that are zero nine days in ten, and the regime dependence of
  Section~\ref{sec:s2results} is read from declared secondary tables and post-hoc stratum tests; no
  registered test supports it until a study is registered for it.
\item \emph{The fourth factorial cell is untested.} The model's own occurrence with a count positive law was
  not registered and is not computed, so the two readings of the accounting trade are not separated here.
\item \emph{Training contamination, the npm categories.} No numerical source or entity overlap is
  established for npm by the reviewed model-card disclosures, training overlap remains unknown, and the
  target blocks of 2026-06-03 to 2026-08-31 postdate the pinned TimesFM 2.5 revision but are not shown to
  postdate the pinned Chronos-2 or TimesFM 3 revisions, so no all-model temporal-clean claim is made; the
  controls that would separate familiarity from skill, the follow-up cohort and the holdout, have not run
  \citep{moghadasi2026, qiao2026, li2026}.
\item \emph{Three models from two families, pinned on 2026-09-12.} No Moirai, TiRex, Toto, Sundial or
  TabPFN-TS variant was run, no demand-specialised model such as EXAONE Demand \citep{exaone2026}, and none
  of the models released since the pins; the pins are registered and cannot change without a new run.
\item \emph{The synthetic grid's limits.} The generator's positive law is a shifted Poisson, the count
  hurdle's own family, so the count hurdle's positive law is correctly specified on the grid, up to its
  estimated mean; its 18 cells cover three zero probabilities, three positive means and two persistences,
  with no overdispersed cell; its families may be familiar in kind to the models, which is not measurable;
  and no transfer to a natural domain follows from it.
\item \emph{One external domain, one horizon pair, three origins.} NYC 311 and Wikipedia are registered
  and not run; the horizons are 7 and 30 days, the 7-day blocks are prefixes of the 30-day blocks, inference
  is conditional on three fixed origins in one summer, and the bootstrap captures between-series variation
  only. The completion of a model's deciles into a count law is a Study 2 assumption, and the discrete CRPS
  is unavailable for the one series whose counts defeat the certified bound.
\item \emph{What a download measures.} npm counts tarball requests, including mirrors and automated
  clients; the series are a directional indicator of adoption, not a count of users.
\end{itemize}

\section{What is registered and not run}
\label{sec:notrun}

\input{shared/s1-followup}

\paragraph{The October holdout.} Study 2's prospective component registered an October 2026 calendar
holdout on the frozen GitHub panel and the npm panel, with a freeze at 2026-09-28 18:00 UTC and a family of
18 tests H01 to H18. The freeze deadline passed with no freeze plan prepared, no forecast generated and no
prediction committed; under the registered rule a late commit fails the prospective gate and no backdating
is allowed. The dated addition of 2026-10-12, item 1, records the holdout as registered and not run, with no
result and no settlement retrieval. A holdout on possibly familiar entities would in any case not replace
Study 1's follow-up cohort or show generalization to unseen ones \citep{moghadasi2026, qiao2026}.

\paragraph{NYC 311 and Wikipedia.} The registered external component had three domains. The dated addition
of 2026-10-12, item 3, decided before any Study 2 target value was accessed, fixes this paper's external
family as the eighteen packages-domain tests E01 to E18 and defers NYC 311 requests and English Wikipedia
pageviews to a later run, where tests E19 to E54 keep their registered definitions and form one family of 36
with its own single adjustment; they are not merged back into a 54-test correction after the eighteen have
been seen. Nothing is claimed about either domain.

\paragraph{The November prospective round.} As of 2026-10-16 a November 2026 prospective round has been
considered and not registered; it can still be registered by its own dated addition before its freeze, and
this sentence is to be re-checked on the posting day. Nothing is claimed about it.
