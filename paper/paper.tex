\begin{abstract}
We ask whether zero-shot time-series foundation models improve probabilistic forecasts of sparse daily
counts. The three studies are registered and population-sampled. Study 1 re-evaluates Chronos-2, TimesFM 3
and TimesFM 2.5 on daily GitHub star counts of 300 repositories sampled from 148,550, against a 28-day
local mean and weekly AutoETS under a nine-decile quantile score. The typical repository favors the local
mean, with the models lower on 46 to 89 of 300 and the signed-rank test on the reference's side in all nine
cells, while the equal-weight mean favors the models in every cell, carried by few repositories; against
AutoETS both summaries favor the models. Study 2 asks whether replacing a frozen model's zero mass with a
28-day context-only occurrence probability, keeping its completed positive law, lowers the score, on 209
hash-sampled npm download series and a 540-series synthetic grid. No pooled test favors the substitution
at $q = 0.05$, and two of six npm tests and all three grid tests reject against it; in the secondary
strata the substitution scores lower for 81, 60 and 66 of the 100 sparse series at horizon 7 and for 32 to
43 of the 100 dense ones. Study 3 registers that reading as a per-stratum family of 36 tests on NYC 311
service requests, 300 series in three strata, with a dynamic occurrence. For dense series the substitution
is worse than the model's own zero
mass: all six tests reject as predicted. The predicted sparse benefit is not established: one of twelve
tests rejects as predicted, and ten of twelve medians are exactly zero, both forecasts putting every decile
at zero on most very sparse series. The dynamic hurdle beats the empirical count hurdle in 14 of 18 tests.
The claim is the registered combination on those three settings, and nothing broader.
\end{abstract}

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{What the three studies say together.} Study 1's post-hoc accounting located the models' mean
gain against the local mean on positive days where the local mean overpredicts, and a zero-day part against
the models in every cell, small beside the mean gain but larger than every median difference. Study 2 asked
whether that zero-day loss can be taken out by a context-only occurrence estimate while the model's
completed positive law is kept, and answered on pooled families: it cannot be taken out without paying more
on positive days, since on the mean, in all nine hurdle-against-control cells, the substitution lowers the
zero-day loss and raises the positive-day underprediction loss by more (Table~\ref{tab:s2parts}), two of six
npm tests and all three synthetic tests reject against it, and no test favors it at $q = 0.05$. That pooled
answer is the dense regime's answer, because 209 equal-weight series on an unscaled score and 18
equal-weight cells let the dense stratum and the short-memory cells decide the families; in the declared
secondary strata the typical sparse npm series favored the substitution at horizon 7 for all three models,
lower on 81, 60 and 66 of 100, and the strata were null at 30 days. Study 3 registered that descriptive
reading as a per-stratum family on a second domain (Section~\ref{sec:s3question}). The dense half held for
every model and horizon on a non-software domain: all six T1 tests reject against the dynamic occurrence,
by about one percent of the control's score, and the occurrence Brier score, a secondary, reads the same
way. The sparse half, read on npm from 81, 60 and 66 of 100 series at horizon 7, did not reproduce as a
confirmatory result on NYC 311, where one of twelve tests rejects as predicted, ten of twelve medians are
exactly zero, and no test rejects the other way. The record supports two readings of that outcome, with
equal weight: the benefit on sparse series may be smaller than the npm secondary tables suggested, or the
registered score may be unable to see it on series whose deciles are all zero under both forecasts, which
is most of the very sparse stratum, where 72 to 88 of the 100 series tie exactly and the secondary Brier
score favors the dynamic occurrence on 68 to 82 of the 100 in every row. Nothing in the record chooses
between the two. The sentence the paper stands behind is therefore conditional, in two parts. For dense
series, a context-only or dynamic occurrence estimate does not improve a frozen model's own completed zero
mass under this score, on npm, the grid and NYC 311, and this is confirmatory on two domains: on npm and
the grid through the pooled families, which the dense series decide, and on NYC 311 through the dense
stratum's own six tests. For sparse series the question is open, with the npm secondary reading on one side
and the NYC 311 family on the other. The model's positive law keeps its value against a count law on this
domain too: carried with the dynamic occurrence it beats the empirical hurdle in 14 of 18 registered tests,
and the four where it does not, R14 and the three very sparse rows at a week, have medians of exactly zero.

\paragraph{Two readings of the accounting trade.} The hurdle's positive law $G_+$ is the model's completed
marginal law truncated at one, not a size forecast the model produced for positive days. A model that
handles zero days by pulling its whole quantile grid down, which is what nine continuous deciles clipped at
zero must do, leaves a truncated law that sits low on positive days; adding context zero mass on top of it
counts the zeros twice, and zero-day loss down with underprediction up by more is exactly what that
predicts. The first reading of the net sign, that the context-only occurrence estimate is worse than the
model's own zero mass, and the second, that the truncated law is not the model's size forecast so that any
added zero mass hurts, are both consistent with every Study 2 number. Study 3 bears on the first and not
on the second. On NYC 311's dense series the dynamic occurrence is worse than the model's own zero mass on
the occurrence Brier score itself, in all six rows with every interval above zero, and the accounting puts
the loss on the zero days, 0.009693 to 0.03213 in every row, with no trade against positive days to explain
(Appendix Table~\ref{tab:s3parts}). The fourth cell, run there as a declared secondary, closes the question
the second reading left open, in the direction the accounting predicted: the model's own occurrence with a
count positive law is far worse than the control on dense series, by 0.2944 to 0.325 on the mean, so the
model's value there sits in its positive law, and against \hc{}, which shares that count positive law, the
model's own occurrence is the better one, on the mean and on 62 to 73 of the 100 dense series. On that
domain the substitution loses on dense series because the context estimate is the worse occurrence
forecast. Whether the double counting of the second reading operates on npm, where the trade ran the other
way, Study 3 does not test, since the fourth cell was run on NYC 311 alone; and the premise remains weaker
than Section~\ref{sec:s2question} might suggest, since on the occurrence Brier score the models' own zero
mass is about as good as the Jeffreys estimate on both Study 2 panels (Appendix Tables~\ref{tab:s2scores}
and~\ref{tab:s2synscores}).

\paragraph{The positive law and the dynamic count.} The second contrast isolates the positive law. On npm
the model's completed positive law beats the shifted Poisson fitted to the last 28 days in all six cells, on
the mean, the median and the rank test; but a Poisson cannot fit a series with thousands of downloads a day,
so on dense series this is a win over a comparator that cannot win, and against the registered empirical
hurdle, read post hoc, the advantage holds at 30 days and on dense series and not at horizon 7 for two of
the three models. On the grid the model's positive law loses to the shifted Poisson for two models and is
not rejected for the third, which leans the same way, where the Poisson is the generator's own law up to its
estimated mean. The registered dynamic count, a four-parameter discounted Bayesian hurdle with no foundation
model, is the best forecaster on the grid after the oracle, lower than each completed model in 18 to 24 of
30 blocks, and it loses to the completed models on 108 to 146 of 209 npm series; with the occurrence held
equal, the foundation models' positive laws underpredict the grid's positive days more than its negative
binomial does. What differs between the two settings is reported, not explained: the grid's positive law is
the count baselines' family and the npm series' is not, and the npm panel spans scales from a median of two
downloads a day to millions where the grid holds $\mu_{+}$ at 1, 5 and 25. On NYC 311 the model's positive
law, carried with the dynamic occurrence, beats the empirical hurdle in 14 of 18 registered tests, in every
dense row, in five of six sparse rows and at 30 days on the very sparse series
(Section~\ref{sec:s3results}); the registered dynamic count itself is scored there as a baseline and not
read against the models beyond the secondary tables of the run.

\paragraph{The raw contrast on a second domain.} On npm the raw deciles beat the local mean on the mean in
all six cells and reject on the rank test in four of six; in Study 1 the rank test favored the reference in
all nine cells, and on the 400 most-starred repositories of a live benchmark the typical series favors the
models \citep{garza2026}. On NYC 311, pooled over the 300 series as a declared secondary, each raw model's
mean is lower than the local mean's in all six rows while the series split close to evenly, 128 to 161
lower against 124 to 157 higher (Appendix Table~\ref{tab:s3pooled}). The designs differ in what is counted,
in how the panel is drawn, in scale, in sparsity, in horizon and in calendar; the paper reports the
differences and does not attribute them. In every study the mean is carried by few series, and the
context-scaled mean, the registered secondary and the summary now recommended in print for intermittent data
\citep{corani2026}, agrees with the medians where the unscaled mean differs.

\paragraph{What the paper claims, and what it does not.} The paper claims the registered combination and
nothing broader: a context-only or dynamic occurrence probability substituted for the zero mass of frozen
Chronos-2 and TimesFM forecasts, their completed positive law kept, judged by a proper quantile score
against a completed-unwrapped control and matched count hurdles, with a dynamic count baseline beside them,
on a hash-sampled panel of daily npm download counts, on the registered synthetic grid and, stratum by
stratum, on a stratified panel of daily NYC 311 service-request counts, with the mean and the typical series
reported side by side; Study 1's GitHub star counts enter through the raw comparison alone, and no
attention-count series went through the hurdle construction. It does not claim to be the first evaluation
of frozen foundation models on sparse daily counts \citep{schoess2026, adler2025, ebrahim2026}, the first
hurdle applied to a frozen model's output \citep{chin2026}, or the first to show that aggregate and
per-series rankings disagree \citep{islam2026, kim2026, brini2026, cherif2026}; a later test window on
familiar entities is not shown here to establish generalization to unseen ones \citep{moghadasi2026,
qiao2026}. The paper does not show that the models forecast bursts, that the substitution would fare the
same on Wikipedia pageviews, which were registered and are not run, that any result transfers to launches or
to the days after a release, that an npm download is a human adoption, or that a 311 request measures the
condition it reports. Study 2's and Study 3's answers are to the registered questions as registered, on the
panels they achieved, with npm's three zero days scored as served and a post-hoc sensitivity beside them.

\section{Limitations}

Study 1's limitations stand as the report gave them; Study 2 and Study 3 add their own.

\begin{itemize}
\input{shared/s1-limitations-items}
\item \emph{Three zero days in the npm targets.} The download API served 0 for all 209 series on
  2026-07-12 and 2026-08-14 and 0 for the largest series on 2026-06-25, between days of millions. The
  registered completeness rule accepted them as explicit counts, the run scored them so, and the dated
  addition of 2026-10-16 keeps that analysis as the confirmatory one; the post-hoc sensitivity changes no
  conclusion without all three days and one row, E06, without the two panel-wide days. Nothing here
  verifies that they are gaps.
\item \emph{One series carries every npm mean.} With equal series weights on the unscaled score, as
  registered, every mean in Table~\ref{tab:s2ext} is a statement about \texttt{aggregate-error}; the
  medians, counts, rank tests and per-stratum counts describe the typical series, and the context-scaled
  score is the registered scale-free secondary.
\item \emph{The npm very sparse stratum holds nine series, and the npm regime reading is secondary.} Only
  9 of 9,998 complete candidates were sparse enough for the third stratum, so Study 2's external test says
  nothing about npm series that are zero nine days in ten, and the regime dependence of
  Section~\ref{sec:s2results} is read from declared secondary tables and post-hoc stratum tests; Study 3
  registered it on NYC 311 and confirmed its dense half only.
\item \emph{The fourth factorial cell was not run on npm or the grid.} The model's own occurrence with a
  count positive law was not registered for Study 2 and is not computed there; it was run on NYC 311 as a
  declared secondary of Study 3, where it bears on the first reading of the accounting trade and not on the
  second.
\item \emph{Training contamination, the npm categories.} No numerical source or entity overlap is
  established for npm by the reviewed model-card disclosures, training overlap remains unknown, and the
  target blocks of 2026-06-03 to 2026-08-31 postdate the pinned TimesFM 2.5 revision but are not shown to
  postdate the pinned Chronos-2 or TimesFM 3 revisions, so no all-model temporal-clean claim is made; the
  controls that would separate familiarity from skill, the follow-up cohort and the holdout, have not run
  \citep{moghadasi2026, qiao2026, li2026}.
\item \emph{Three models from two families, pinned on 2026-09-12.} No Moirai, TiRex, Toto, Sundial or
  TabPFN-TS variant was run, no demand-specialised model such as EXAONE Demand \citep{exaone2026}, and none
  of the models released since the pins; the pins are registered and cannot change without a new run.
\item \emph{The synthetic grid's limits.} The generator's positive law is a shifted Poisson, the count
  hurdle's own family, so the count hurdle's positive law is correctly specified on the grid, up to its
  estimated mean; its 18 cells cover three zero probabilities, three positive means and two persistences,
  with no overdispersed cell; its families may be familiar in kind to the models, which is not measurable;
  and no transfer to a natural domain follows from it.
\item \emph{Two natural domains, one horizon pair, three origins.} Study 2's deferred NYC 311 and
  Wikipedia tests, E19 to E54, are registered and not run, and Study 3's NYC 311 family is its own; the
  horizons are 7 and 30 days, the 7-day blocks are prefixes of the 30-day blocks, inference is conditional
  on three fixed origins in one summer, and the bootstrap captures between-series variation only. The
  completion of a model's deciles into a count law is a Study 2 assumption, and the discrete CRPS is
  unavailable for the one npm series whose counts defeat the certified bound.
\item \emph{What a download measures.} npm counts tarball requests, including mirrors and automated
  clients; the series are a directional indicator of adoption, not a count of users.
\item \emph{The registered score is blind below one decile.} \qs{} reads nine integer deciles, so an
  occurrence probability that moves by less than one decile leaves the score unchanged, and two forecasts
  whose deciles coincide score identically whatever the target. On the very sparse NYC 311 series both
  forecasts put every decile at zero in every window for 71 to 87 of the tied series in each T1 row, so 72
  to 88 of the 100 series tie exactly where the secondary Brier score does not tie once. ``Sparse'' in H3's
  sense is therefore a regime the registered score can test only where the substitution moves a decile; on
  series that are zero nine days in ten it mostly does not, and the family cannot tell a small benefit from
  none. The very sparse means ride on one series, \texttt{Sewer Maintenance} in community board
  \texttt{09 QUEENS}, whose omission moves the mean most in all six T1 rows.
\item \emph{The signed-rank test with many exact ties.} The registered inference omits zero differences,
  the zero rule the inference command declares, and reports $p = 1$ when all are zero; with 72 to 88 ties a
  very sparse test runs on 12 to 28 series, its location reading needs symmetry, and it is not a test of the
  mean. The direction is read from the rank sums, as registered.
\item \emph{The targets, the terms and the scope.} The targets were read once, 47 days after the last
  target day, from a dataset updated daily by automation, with no check for later revision. The dataset
  states no license, the city website's terms of use, read on 2026-10-17, are silent on datasets, and the
  Open Data Law the program names as its basis was not read; the records name the source and claim no
  endorsement. A 311 request is a complaint or inquiry about a city service, a third kind of count beside
  stars and downloads and not a measure of the condition it reports; the claim's scope is the three settings
  the studies ran on, and nothing broader.
\item \emph{Not in the tree, and contamination.} The 300 NYC 311 checkpoints (645 MB), the raw captures
  and the target signals are not in the repository; the checkpoints are to be published as a release asset,
  and the inference and integrity outputs, the panel files and the results page are committed. Under Study
  2's ledger, as computed for NYC 311: no exact source or entity overlap is established in the reviewed
  disclosures; civic data may occur in broadly described training corpora, and absence of disclosure is not
  absence from training; the retrospective calendar does not certify that the 2026 target days postdate the
  availability of all three checkpoints.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{The October holdout.} Study 2's prospective component registered an October 2026 calendar
holdout on the frozen GitHub panel and the npm panel, with a freeze at 2026-09-28 18:00 UTC and a family of
18 tests H01 to H18. The freeze deadline passed with no freeze plan prepared, no forecast generated and no
prediction committed; under the registered rule a late commit fails the prospective gate and no backdating
is allowed. The dated addition of 2026-10-12, item 1, records the holdout as registered and not run, with no
result and no settlement retrieval. A holdout on possibly familiar entities would in any case not replace
Study 1's follow-up cohort or show generalization to unseen ones \citep{moghadasi2026, qiao2026}.

\paragraph{Study 2's deferred domains.} The registered external component had three domains. The dated
addition of 2026-10-12, item 3, decided before any Study 2 target value was accessed, fixes Study 2's
external family as the eighteen packages-domain tests E01 to E18 and defers NYC 311 requests and English
Wikipedia pageviews to a later run, where tests E19 to E54 keep their registered definitions and form one
family of 36 with its own single adjustment; they are not merged back into a 54-test correction after the
eighteen have been seen. Study 3 ran on NYC 311 under its own registration and its own family, R01 to R36,
and adds no test to E19 to E54, which remain registered and not run; Wikipedia is registered and not run.
Nothing is claimed about Study 2's deferred tests or about Wikipedia.

\paragraph{The November prospective round.} As of 2026-10-16 a November 2026 prospective round has been
considered and not registered; it can still be registered by its own dated addition before its freeze, and
this sentence is to be re-checked on the posting day. Nothing is claimed about it.
