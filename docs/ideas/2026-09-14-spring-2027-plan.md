## 1. Rolling monthly prospective benchmark

### What it is

One forecast origin per calendar month, on the same frozen panels, with the
forecasts committed to git and pushed before the first target day. Scores are
computed after a declared settlement lag. The October 2026 round is Study 2's
holdout (plan step C, frozen by 2026-09-28). This plan continues it monthly.

The value is temporal: every target day is later than every frozen checkpoint
and later than the committed forecasts, so no model could have seen the
targets. It does not test unseen entities; the panels are the same series
each month. Say this in every document that reports it.

### Panels

| Domain | Panel | Source of manifest |
|---|---|---|
| GitHub | The 300 Study 1 repositories, same manifest | `reports/registered-2026-09-13/run/manifest.json` |
| npm | The 300 Study 2 packages | Study 2 manifest, once it exists |
| NYC 311 | The 300 Study 2 problem x community-board cells | Study 2 manifest, once it exists |

If 311 was cut from Study 2, the benchmark starts with two domains and 311
joins in the first month after its panel is frozen. Record the month it
joined. No panel is resampled or topped up. A series whose entity disappears
stays in the panel; its missing targets are recorded as missing, not zero, and
the comparison for that series is dropped for that month with the count
reported.

Rules behind the table:

- Context ends the day before the freeze deadline, so the freeze commit
  happens with a three-day gap before the first target. The gap absorbs the
  star-history endpoint's and npm's reporting delay and gives the author a
  day to paste the commit. If any source cannot deliver context through that
  date, the round uses the last complete day and records the shortfall; it
  does not move the target window.
- Targets are 30 consecutive days from the first of the month. Horizons are
  7 and 30 days from the same origin, as in the October holdout. No 90-day
  horizon; the elapsed time is not available.
- Scoring waits 15 days after the last target day. The lag exists for 311,
  where records are created with delay and revised; for GitHub, where unstars
  and account removals revise history; and for npm, where the daily count is
  final within a day or two. One lag for all three keeps the calendar simple.
  Retrieval date and response hashes are recorded at scoring, and the same
  retrieval is never repeated to get a different number.
- Seven rounds through April 2027 are declared up front. The pooled analysis
  in section 1.5 uses exactly these rounds; nothing is added because a month
  looked interesting, and no month is dropped because it was dormant.

### What is frozen each round

The same models and baselines as Study 2, same revisions, same runtime pins,
same settings. The hurdle variants and the dynamic count baseline are
included from the first round in which they exist in a registered section.
The fine-tuned model, if it exists, joins from the round named in its own
registration (section 3), with its weight SHA-256 committed before that
round's freeze. No other model is added mid-series.

Each round commits, before the freeze deadline:

- `reports/rolling/<YYYY-MM>/forecasts.parquet`: every series, model,
  horizon, target day and decile. About 900 series x 18 to 24 models x 30 days
  x 9 deciles; a few megabytes. Commit the file itself, not only its hash.
- `reports/rolling/<YYYY-MM>/freeze.json`: context end date per domain,
  retrieval times, response hashes, model revisions, seeds, the SHA-256 of
  `forecasts.parquet`, and the git hash of the previous round's scoring
  commit.
- The commit is pushed to origin the same day. The push is the timestamp;
  local commit dates are author-settable and prove nothing on their own.

After scoring, a second commit adds `targets.parquet`, `scores.parquet`,
`scoring.json` with retrieval hashes and the per-series missingness table,
and one summary CSV per domain in the Study 2 table format.

### Inference

Declared once, in the rolling benchmark's protocol section, before the
November round:

- Per round and per domain, the Study 2 comparison family and its BH
  correction, reported as descriptive replication of that family. The
  confirmatory family for the whole series is the pooled analysis below.
- Pooled over the seven rounds, per domain: average each series' score over
  rounds it has targets for, then the same repository-cluster bootstrap and
  signed-rank tests as Study 2, one family. Months are not independent
  draws of the calendar and the bootstrap does not pretend they are; say
  that the pooled result is conditional on these seven months.
- A month-by-month table of mean and median paired differences and win
  fractions, so a reader can see whether the pattern holds across calendar
  conditions. This is descriptive.

### Effort

| Item | Per round |
|---|---|
| GPU | 3 to 10 hours across three domains at one origin, from the memo's per-origin estimate |
| Author | One command, one commit block, one push at freeze; the same at scoring |
| Check | The freeze commit landed before the first target day; the round's summary recomputed from `scores.parquet` |

Cost is the elapsed calendar, not compute. The one failure mode that matters:
a freeze commit dated on or after the first target day. That round is then
reported as not prospective, with the reason. It is not regenerated.

## 2. Registered follow-up cohort: repositories created on or after 2026-09-13

The protocol section "Registered follow-up after the write-up" fixes the
design: identical eligibility, strata, sample size, models, baselines, scores,
regime rules and relative window calendar; every repository gets 90 context
days plus 270 target days; the creation-window closing date, endpoint and
frame coverage are registered before collection. Three things are open and
must be settled in the dated section before any data pull.

### 2.1 The frame source, because GH Archive WatchEvents collapsed

Study 1's eligibility, at least 20 WatchEvents over days 1 to 90, came from
GH Archive, and Study 1's creation window was closed at 2025-02-21 exactly so
that every day 90 fell before the capture collapse. A 2026 cohort cannot use
that count unless capture has recovered. First action, October 2026:

- Check the state of [gharchive.org issue 320] and measure WatchEvent capture
  for September 2026 against the star-history endpoint for the 20 Study 1
  validation repositories, the same comparison the protocol used for 2024.
  Bounded query; dry-run cost first as usual.
- If capture is back to 2024 levels, the frame is built exactly as in Study 1.
- If not, the frame is CreateEvents from GH Archive, if those are still
  captured, or the GitHub Search API sliced by creation hour, and eligibility
  is measured with the star-history endpoint at day 90. That changes the
  eligibility measure from gross events to surviving stars, which Study 1
  measured as 3 to 8 percent lower for popular repositories. State the change
  in the registration; do not call the two cohorts identically sampled.

Whatever the frame, the census is context-only: it reads nothing after each
repository's day 90.

### 2.2 The creation window, because the top stratum is thin

Study 1's frame had 1,052 repositories with at least 1,000 events by day 90
over a 782-day creation window, about 1.3 per day, and 24 of the first 124
walked were gone at fetch time. The window length sets both the top-stratum
size and the maturity date.

| Window closes | Days | Expected 1000+ eligible | After 20 percent skips | Last day 90 | Earliest endpoint E |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2026-09-30 | 18 | 24 | 19 | 2026-12-28 | 2027-09-24 |
| 2026-10-31 | 49 | 66 | 53 | 2027-01-28 | 2027-10-25 |
| 2026-11-30 | 79 | 106 | 85 | 2027-02-27 | 2027-11-24 |
| 2026-12-21 | 100 | 135 | 108 | 2027-03-20 | 2027-12-15 |

Recommendation: close the window on 2026-10-31 and accept a top stratum of
about 50. The protocol already says an exhausted stratum keeps what it has
and transfers no slots. A September 2027 maturity, which earlier notes
assumed, buys an 18-day window and a top stratum near 20, which is too few
to say anything about the stratum that carried Study 1's mean. Pushing to
December 2027 for a full 100 costs two more months of waiting on a study whose
result will already be a year after the paper. The two lower strata fill in
any of these windows. This is a decision for the author to register.

## 3. Bounded fine-tune, if it did not fit before November

Runs in spring only if the fine-tune was not done by 2026-10-25, which it was not. If it was, this section is already done and the journal version
reports it.

### Design constraints, all fixed in its own protocol section

- One model. Default Chronos-2: Apache-2.0 weights allow a derived
  checkpoint to be released; TimesFM 3's noncommercial licence would need
  checking before any derived weights are published. Confirm the pinned
  package version documents a fine-tuning path before registering; if it
  does not, TimesFM 2.5 is the fallback for the same licence reason.
- Development data: a fresh hash sample of entities, disjoint from the Study
  1 panel, the Study 2 panels and the follow-up cohort, from the same frames
  and strata rules. Development targets end on or before 2026-12-31. Both
  splits are by entity and by calendar. Nothing from a test panel or a test
  month touches the fine-tune.
- Test: the fine-tuned model enters the rolling benchmark from the March
  2027 round, with its weight SHA-256 committed before the 2027-02-25 freeze.
  It is compared with the frozen base model on the same family; that adds
  one model to the round's family from that month and is declared then.
- Budget: at most 40 GPU-hours total, one learning rate and one epoch count
  chosen on the development split only, no search over more than four
  configurations, all four recorded. The bound is a rule, not an estimate.
- Report either way. A fine-tune that hurts the typical series or overfits
  the development domain is a result.

## 4. The journal version

The November deliverable is the preprint and the TMLR submission. TMLR's
cycle returns reviews in roughly two months, so a revision falls in January
to March 2027. The spring version is that revision. What it adds beyond
review responses:

- The rolling rounds settled by the revision deadline: November through
  January at the earliest, through March if the deadline slips. Report the
  month-by-month table and the pooled family only if all seven declared
  rounds are in; otherwise report the rounds as descriptive and say the
  pooled analysis is pending.
- The fine-tune result, if section 3 ran and its test rounds are scored.
- Nothing from the follow-up cohort; it matures in October 2027 and goes in
  a separate short report or a later version, stated as such in the paper.

What it does not do: reopen Study 1, change any registered family, or add a
model to the rolling series. If a reviewer asks for a model the series does
not include, the answer is a new round with a declared addition, not a rerun.

If TMLR rejects, the same revision goes to the next venue on the memo's list
whose rules allow it, with the rolling rounds as the added evidence.
